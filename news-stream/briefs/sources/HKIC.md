# Background brief: Hong Kong Investment Corporation Limited (香港投資管理有限公司, 港投公司, HKIC)

*Facts as of 22 October 2026. Numbers in square brackets refer to the source list at the end.*

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

The Hong Kong Investment Corporation Limited (HKIC) is a company wholly owned by the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) [1]. It invests public money, from Hong Kong's fiscal reserves, in companies and funds. It has two stated goals (its "dual mandate"): to earn a "reasonable financial return over the medium to long term" and to strengthen Hong Kong's long-term competitiveness and economic vitality [1]. It describes itself as "Patient Capital" (耐心資本) and as the government's "patient capital investment flagship" [6][26]. Its starting capital was HK$62 billion (about US$8 billion), made up of four government funds [1][34]. Its current focus areas are Hard and Core Technology, Biotechnology and Health Technology, New Energy and Green Technology, and related applications [1]. Its board is chaired by Hong Kong's Financial Secretary, Paul Chan Mo-po (陳茂波) [3][4], and its Chief Executive Officer is Clara Chan Ka-chai (陳家齊), in post since 9 October 2023 and reappointed for three more years from 9 October 2026 [5][7].

## 3. Where the money came from: the funds it manages

HKIC manages four pools of money, which together make up the HK$62 billion starting capital [1][15]:

| Fund | Chinese | Size | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hong Kong Growth Portfolio (HKGP) | 香港增長組合 | HK$22 billion | Announced in the 2020-21 Budget: 10% of the government's Future Fund, for strategic investments in projects "with a Hong Kong angle" [2][17][19] |
| Greater Bay Area Investment Fund | 大灣區投資基金 | HK$5 billion | 2022-23 Budget added HK$10 billion to the HKGP; half went to this fund [2][73] |
| Strategic Tech Fund | 策略性創科基金 | HK$5 billion | The other half of the 2022-23 Budget top-up [2][73] |
| Co-Investment Fund | 共同投資基金 | HK$30 billion | Announced in the Chief Executive's 2022 Policy Address, taken from the Future Fund, to attract companies to set up in Hong Kong [2][84] |

**Background on the Future Fund (未來基金).** The Future Fund was set up from part of Hong Kong's fiscal reserves after the February 2015 Budget, as long-term savings placed with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA, 香港金融管理局) and invested partly through the Exchange Fund's Long-Term Growth Portfolio [82]. The HKGP was carved out of it in 2020 [19].

**The HKGP before HKIC.** In September 2020 the government set up a two-tier structure for the HKGP: a Governance Committee chaired by the Financial Secretary and an Investment Committee chaired by the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury, with the HKMA doing the executive work [18]. In September 2021 it appointed the first three private equity firms as general partners (GPs) to invest for the HKGP [18]; by the end of 2022 eight GPs had been appointed [17]. In January 2025 a legislator asked about reports that some of those GPs had not yet invested or set up teams in Hong Kong [17]. Management of the HKGP has since been transferred to HKIC [2].

**Further capital.** In the 2026-27 Budget (25 February 2026), the Financial Secretary said the HK$62 billion had been "largely allocated" (大致分配) and that the government would inject capital "in a timely manner" (適時注資) [32]. In June 2026 Clara Chan said HKIC was discussing a further injection with the government [34], and Paul Chan said part of an injection might fund an offshore renminbi venture capital fund [35]. In a LegCo paper dated 28 May 2026 the government said it would decide on a further injection after reviewing HKIC's progress and taking account of the views of the community and the Legislative Council [12]. No amount for a new injection had been announced in the sources found up to 22 October 2026.

**HKIC also runs a fifth, separate pot.** Since 2024 HKIC sets up and oversees the Investment Portfolio (CIES IP) of the New Capital Investment Entrant Scheme (新資本投資者入境計劃, "New CIES"), a residency-by-investment scheme. Each applicant must invest at least HK$30 million, of which HK$3 million goes into the CIES IP [58]. HKIC does not invest this money itself; it selects outside asset managers (see section 7) [58][59].

## 6. Management and organisation

**Clara Chan Ka-chai (陳家齊), CEO.** She is a barrister admitted by the High Court of Hong Kong. She was a government Administrative Officer from 2004 to 2010 (home affairs, transport and financial services), joined the HKMA in 2010, became Chief Investment Officer (Private Markets) in the Exchange Fund Investment Office in 2018 and Executive Director (Monetary Management) in 2020 [5]. At her appointment she co-chaired a World Economic Forum Global Future Council on Responsible Investing and sat on the Council of Lingnan University [5]. Her 2024 salary was HK$7.2 million [24]. Ta Kung Pao calculated that this is about HK$1 million more than the average pay of an HKMA executive director in 2023 (HK$6.173 million) [25]. In announcing her reappointment, Paul Chan said her results were "plain for all to see" (成績有目共睹) [7].

**Staff and costs.** As of April 2025 HKIC had four departments (Investment, Risk and Compliance, Legal, Corporate Affairs) and a headcount of 53 [13][25]. Its 2024 staff-related costs were HK$43 million, and staff at managing director level or above averaged HK$4.7 million a year [24]. Its first pay review, covering 2024 performance bonuses, was due in 2025 and is decided by the Remuneration Committee and the Board [24][25]. HKIC describes a "seven-step" investment process and "three lines of defence" risk management [24]. In October 2026 it opened applications for its first internship programme [39].

**Office and contacts.** Suite 909, 9/F, One Pacific Place, 88 Queensway, Admiralty; tel. 2328 2625; enquiry@hkic.org.hk; proposals to submission@hkic.org.hk [76][77].

## 7. Strategy and how it invests

- **Themes.** Hard and Core Technology; Biotechnology and Health Technology; New Energy and Green Technology; and related applications [1]. The 2025 Annual Report names brain-computer interfaces, commercial aerospace, and digital and financial infrastructure as areas it will expand into [26]. The 2025 Policy Address tasked it with investing in AI, cross-border biotech with the Guangdong government, local private equity and hedge fund managers, and commercial aerospace [44].
- **Methods.** HKIC mainly makes direct investments and co-investments, as equity, debt or both [13]. Investee companies must contribute to Hong Kong: for example by opening offices, hiring talent, setting up R&D or corporate venture capital arms in Hong Kong, and choosing Hong Kong first for a stock listing [13][15]. Companies headquartered outside Hong Kong must do core R&D, hold IP or list in Hong Kong [12].
- **"Invest early, invest small."** Clara Chan calls 「投早投小」 HKIC's core strategy and says it carries risk [36]. HKIC says about 70% of its capital has gone into early-stage and growth-stage companies [40][71].
- **Roles.** Clara Chan says HKIC has moved from being a "pure investor" to also acting as lead investor, enabler, accelerator and "first mover" (先行者) [37][38].
- **Holding after listing.** She has said that a company's listing does not necessarily mean HKIC will sell out [81b]. (Source: Ming Pao, 4 Oct 2026.)
- **Leverage claim.** HKIC reports how much other long-term capital invests alongside each dollar it puts in: over HK$4 (March 2025) [15], over HK$6 (June 2025) [21], over HK$8 (February 2026 onward) [32][26]. The co-investors include overseas sovereign wealth funds and pension funds [26].
- **Commercial property.** The 2026-27 Budget said HKIC would work with regional and international long-term capital to put money into commercial property that fits Hong Kong's industrial priorities [32][33]. HKET reported that this was expected to include offices [81].
- **Offshore renminbi venture capital fund (離岸人民幣創投基金).** First described in June 2026 [34][35] and in the 2025 Annual Report [26]. On 4 October 2026 Clara Chan said it had started investing, in AI and brain-computer-interface projects, and that she believed it was the world's first such fund. She also said HKIC would build a bond-investment line with Hong Kong banks, with details still to be announced [36]. HKIC has called the fund "the world's first offshore renminbi venture capital fund with a focus on frontier technology" [41].
- **Northern Metropolis and GBA.** The 2026 Policy Address says HKIC will lead investment that helps companies set up in the Northern Metropolis (北部都會區) and other Greater Bay Area cities [43], and will help start-ups set up in Nansha (南沙) [43]. HKIC names Qianhai (前海), Nansha and the Hetao (河套) zone as platforms [41].
- **National framing.** HKIC's 2026 statements tie its work to the national 15th Five-Year Plan (「十五五」), "New Quality Productive Forces" (新質生產力) and technological self-reliance [6][26]. Hong Kong's First Five-Year Plan says the government will use HKIC as patient capital for co-investment in its focus sectors [41].

**New CIES Investment Portfolio managers.** For the 2024 batch (at least HK$300 million expected), HKIC appointed Betatron Venture Group, Inno Angel Fund, MindWorks Capital and Radiant Tech Ventures, with BOCI-Prudential Trustee as administrator, KPMG supervising the selection and Clifford Chance as legal adviser [58][59]. For the 2025 batch it appointed ten: Abax Global Capital, Beyond Ventures, CMC Capital, FirstLight Capital, Hidden Hill Capital, M Capital, Polymer Capital, Primavera Capital, Trustar Capital/CITIC Capital with Vision Capital Investment Management, and Value Partners [60]. The 2026 batch opened for proposals on 12 October 2026, with investment expected to start in the first quarter of 2027 [61]. Clara Chan said the 2026 batch would total at least HK$4 billion [39].

## 11. Debates, criticism and open issues

**Transparency.** Members of the Legislative Council (LegCo, 立法會), including pro-establishment members, have repeatedly asked for more disclosure:
- November 2023: Rock Chen (陳恒鑌) said "there are views that the operation of the Corporation lacks transparency". The government replied that investment details were market-sensitive and would not be disclosed [16].
- July 2024: Regina Ip (葉劉淑儀) asked how much HKIC had invested in SmartMore. The government said confidentiality clauses prevented disclosure, and that HKIC would make "appropriate disclosure" in its annual report [14]. She also asked why HKIC had first posted only a Chinese-language press release [14].
- April 2025: Adrian Ho (何敬康) asked for the names, sectors, stakes and expected returns of HKIC's roughly 100 investments, for senior staff pay and for performance indicators. The reply gave theme percentages and the headcount but no list of investments [13].
- A December 2025 article on KongHub said there is still no full public list of HKIC's investments, amounts or shareholdings, and that the government cites "commercial sensitivity" [75].
- The first annual report (December 2025) disclosed the CEO's salary and staff costs [24].

**Performance measures.** LegCo members asked for key performance indicators beyond financial return. HKIC replied that it should not be judged on financial returns alone, and pointed to its job, floor-space and IP figures [12]. In 2024 the government said the Board's targets for the CEO were mainly to build governance, the team and networks, and to find investments [14].

**How much goes to Hong Kong.** Members said that Hong Kong companies making up just over 30% of investments was "less than ideal", and urged more local investment, including requiring investees to keep production lines in Hong Kong. HKIC replied that the Hong Kong market is too small to restrict all investment to Hong Kong [12]. Members also raised the worry that if investees do R&D in Hong Kong but manufacture elsewhere, this could lead to "deindustrialization" [12].

**Crowding out private investors.** Members warned that large direct investments could crowd out private venture funds, which they described as government "competing with the people for profit" (與民爭利). HKIC replied that it fills gaps that private funds overlook, by investing earlier [12].

**Comparison with Temasek.** A March 2025 LegCo question asked whether HKIC would learn from Temasek [15]. In June 2024 Paul Chan said the comparison was "apples and oranges", because HKIC must also drive industrial development [72]. A July 2024 Master Insight column also argued that HKIC is not a Hong Kong Temasek [73].

**More capital.** Whether and how much to inject is still being decided; see section 3 [12][32][34].

**HKGP general partners.** In January 2025 a legislator asked whether money should be taken back from HKGP general partners who had not invested or set up in Hong Kong. The government said HKGP returns would be disclosed five years after it started operating [17].

## 12. Easily confused with

- **China Investment Corporation (中國投資有限責任公司, 中投公司, CIC):** the mainland's national sovereign wealth fund, based in Beijing. Its English and Chinese names are similar to HKIC's, but it is a separate body; none of the HKIC sources above mention any link (Claude's own background knowledge, not from a cited source).
- **Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA, 金管局) and the Exchange Fund (外匯基金):** the central-bank-like body and its investment fund. They manage the Future Fund and supported HKIC at its start, but they are separate from HKIC [8][82].
- **Future Fund (未來基金):** the long-term pool of reserves that HKIC's funds were taken from [82][19].
- **Hong Kong Growth Portfolio (香港增長組合):** one of the four funds HKIC manages, not another name for HKIC [2].
- **Co-Investment Fund (共同投資基金):** a HK$30 billion component of HKIC, not a separate body [2].
- **I&T Industry-Oriented Fund (創科產業引導基金):** a separate government innovation fund. The 2026 Policy Address said it was expected to start within the year, with about 20 fund managers shortlisted [85].
- **Office for Attracting Strategic Enterprises (OASES, 引進重點企業辦公室) and Invest Hong Kong (投資推廣署):** government units that attract companies to Hong Kong. The Financial Secretary's Office coordinates them with HKIC [12].
- **Old and new CIES:** HKIC oversees only the Investment Portfolio of the *New* Capital Investment Entrant Scheme (2024–) [58].
- **Temasek (淡馬錫):** Singapore's state investor, the model behind the nickname 「港版淡馬錫」 [72].
- **Clara Chan (陳家齊) vs Paul Chan (陳茂波):** both have the surname Chan (陳) and both appear at HKIC events. Paul Chan is the Financial Secretary and HKIC chairman; Clara Chan is the CEO [3][7].
