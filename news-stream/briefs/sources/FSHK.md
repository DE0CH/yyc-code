# Background brief: Hong Kong's Financial Secretary Paul Chan Mo-po (陳茂波), his deputy, his office and the 2026-27 Budget

*Status as of 22 October 2026. Every factual statement carries a numbered citation; the source list is at the end. Where something could not be confirmed, the brief says so.*

---

## 1. What the subject is

The **Financial Secretary** (財政司司長) is one of the three "Secretaries of Department" at the top of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) government, ranked alongside the Chief Secretary for Administration (政務司司長) and the Secretary for Justice (律政司司長) [6]. Officially, the Financial Secretary's main job is to assist the Chief Executive (行政長官) in overseeing financial, monetary, economic, trade, development, housing, transport, logistics, innovation and technology, and industrial policy. He controls the **Exchange Fund** (外匯基金) with the help of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA, 香港金融管理局), and he sits on the Executive Council (行政會議, ExCo), which is the cabinet [2]. The Exchange Fund Ordinance places the Fund under the Financial Secretary's control, exercised in consultation with an Exchange Fund Advisory Committee [63].

The Financial Secretary is also in charge of the government **Budget** (財政預算案). Under the Public Finance Ordinance he lays the government's annual revenue and expenditure estimates before the Legislative Council (立法會, LegCo), delivers the annual Budget Speech, and moves the **Appropriation Bill** (撥款條例草案), which gives the spending proposals legal effect [2]. Hong Kong's financial year runs from 1 April to 31 March. Government accounts report fiscal reserves "at 31 March" each year [22].

The current Financial Secretary is **Paul Chan Mo-po** (陳茂波). He has held the post since 16 January 2017 and is the fifth person to hold it since 1997 [4][5]. His deputy since 1 July 2022 is the **Deputy Financial Secretary Michael Wong Wai-lun** (黃偉綸) [1][8].

## 3. Paul Chan Mo-po: biography

- **Birth and family.** Born 18 March 1955 in Hong Kong, so he is 71 [4]. Chinese Wikipedia gives his ancestral home as Chaozhou (潮州), Guangdong [5]. He grew up poor in a squatter area at Nam Shan (南山) in Shek Kip Mei (石硤尾). A US Army lieutenant sponsored him as a child through a foster-parent charity [5]. Chinese Wikipedia also states that the father of banker David Li Kwok-po (李國寶), the accountant Li Fook-shu (李福樹), paid his university fees [5].
- **Education.** He holds a BBA and an MBA from the Chinese University of Hong Kong (香港中文大學) and completed executive programmes at Harvard Business School [1]. Chinese Wikipedia says he graduated in accounting from CUHK's New Asia College in 1977 and took his MBA in 1984 [5].
- **Career before government.** He is a certified public accountant [1]. He worked at the Inland Revenue Department and the Construction Industry Training Authority, and set up his own accounting firm in 1990 [5]. He was Chairman of the Association of Chartered Certified Accountants (ACCA) Hong Kong and President of the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA, 香港會計師公會) [1]. Chinese Wikipedia dates these to 1996-97 and 2006 [5]. He also chaired the Legal Aid Services Council (法律援助服務局) [1].
- **Legislator.** He represented the Accountancy functional constituency in LegCo from 1 October 2008 until he resigned on 29 July 2012 to join the government [4][5].
- **Secretary for Development** (發展局局長), 30 July 2012 to 16 January 2017, under Chief Executive Leung Chun-ying (梁振英). He replaced Mak Chai-kwong (麥齊光), who had resigned over a housing-allowance scandal [4].
- **Financial Secretary since 16 January 2017.** He succeeded John Tsang Chun-wah (曾俊華), who resigned to run for Chief Executive [4][5]. He was reappointed under Carrie Lam (林鄭月娥) and again in June 2022 under John Lee Ka-chiu (李家超) [5].
- **Other roles.** Member of the Committee for Safeguarding National Security of the HKSAR since July 2020 [4][5]. Chairman of the Candidate Eligibility Review Committee (候選人資格審查委員會) from 14 April to 19 September 2022, taking over from John Lee when Lee left government to run for Chief Executive [5].
- **Honours.** Grand Bauhinia Medal (GBM, 大紫荊勳賢), 2017 [4]. His full styling is "Mr Paul Chan, GBM, GBS, MH, JP" [6].
- **Personal.** He is married to Frieda Hui Po-ming (許步明) and they have a son and a daughter [4]. He is a Christian [5] and a voting member of the Hong Kong Jockey Club [4].

## 4. The Deputy Financial Secretary: Michael Wong Wai-lun (黃偉綸)

- **The post.** It was created in July 2022 under John Lee's government restructuring, together with a Deputy Chief Secretary and a Deputy Secretary for Justice [7]. Officially the Deputy helps the Financial Secretary coordinate cross-bureau policy and supervise his bureaux. He sits on ExCo, takes charge of projects assigned by the Chief Executive and the Financial Secretary, and deputises when the Financial Secretary cannot perform his duties [2].
- **Career.** Born 1962. He holds a BSocSc from the University of Hong Kong and an LLB from the University of London (external programme) [9]. He joined the Administrative Service in 1985 and has been Director of Information Services (2009), Director of Marine (2014), Permanent Secretary for Development (Planning and Lands) (2015-17) and Secretary for Development (July 2017 to June 2022). He has been Deputy Financial Secretary since 1 July 2022 [8]. His wife, May Chan Wing-shiu, is a civil servant who became Commissioner for Labour in September 2022 [9].
- **Assigned portfolios.**
  - He heads the Working Group on Developing Low-altitude Economy (低空經濟), which held its first meeting on 12 November 2024 [71].
  - After the November 2025 Tai Po fire he headed the Emergency Accommodation Arrangements task force (應急住宿安排工作組) [55].
  - He announced the government's offer of up to HK$6.8 billion to buy out the titles of Wang Fuk Court owners [15]. In September 2026 he said 99.3% of owners had accepted, more than 88% had signed sale contracts, and the government did not rule out legislating in 2027 to resume the remaining titles [37].
- **Acting Financial Secretary.** He acts whenever Chan is abroad, for example during Chan's trips to Bangkok (15 to 17 October 2026) and Beijing (22 to 24 October 2026) [41][42]. In July 2026, as Acting Financial Secretary, he wrote a Sing Tao Daily article rebutting critics who said Hong Kong's prospects were dim. He said some critics seemed driven "by bias against China" [56]. The article responded to economist Stephen Roach's Project Syndicate column "Yes, the Hong Kong of Old Is Over" (23 July 2026) [9].
- **Recent statements.** On 23 September 2026 he said on Metro Radio that:
  - bond proceeds would be used only for the Northern Metropolis and capital works;
  - government spending is about 25% of GDP and the aim is to bring it down to 23% or 22%;
  - the gold central clearing system is expected to start in the first quarter of 2027;
  - civil servants will continue to be paid in Hong Kong dollars [37].

**Committees chaired by the Financial Secretary in 2026**

- The new **Committee on AI+ and Industry Development Strategy** (「AI+與產業發展策略委員會」), announced in the 2026-27 Budget [11][14].
- The Inter-departmental Task Force on Monitoring Fuel Supply. During the 2026 oil price spike it proposed diesel subsidies and halved tolls at government tunnels [31].

**Policy areas that appear most often with Chan's name in 2025-26 coverage**

- Budget and public finance: deficits, bonds, the Exchange Fund transfer (Section 7).
- The **Northern Metropolis** (北部都會區), the development zone bordering Shenzhen. Chan has called it a major growth engine for the "next decade or two" [38]. Components include:
  - the Hetao (河套) Shenzhen-Hong Kong Science and Technology Innovation Co-operation Zone's Hong Kong Park (the Loop);
  - San Tin Technopole (新田科技城);
  - Hung Shui Kiu (洪水橋) [11][14].
- Financial-centre initiatives:
  - gold trading: a central clearing pilot and a memorandum of understanding with the Shanghai Gold Exchange [46];
  - stablecoin licensing under the Stablecoins Ordinance, which took effect on 1 August 2025. The HKMA granted the first two licences on 10 April 2026, to HSBC and to Anchorpoint Financial (a Standard Chartered, HKT and Animoca Brands joint venture) [47];
  - offshore renminbi business;
  - retail "Silver Bonds". The August 2026 batch targeted HK$50 billion, with a minimum 4.25% interest rate, for residents born in 1967 or earlier [48].
- Attracting enterprises (OASES), and AI policy through "AI+" [11][43].

## 6. Controversies from his time as a legislator and Development Secretary

These episodes still come up in forum posts:

- **Subdivided flats (劏房) affair, July-August 2012.** Weeks after Chan became Development Secretary, newspapers reported that Harvest Charm Development (景捷發展), a company his wife held and of which both had been directors, owned old flats in Tai Kok Tsui (大角咀) that had been subdivided and let. Chan first said he knew nothing about the subdivided flats, then said on 5 August that he had known of sub-letting when the flats were bought [4][5]. Pro-establishment parties DAB (民建聯) and FTU (工聯會) asked him to explain, and pan-democrats called for his resignation [59].
- **Kwu Tung (古洞) farmland, July 2013.** Apple Daily reported that a company linked to his wife's family held farmland inside the North East New Territories development area, which his bureau was pushing forward. Chan said the land was bought in 1994 for leisure use and that he had declared it to ExCo. He refused calls to recuse himself [4]. On 26 July 2013 the LegCo Panel on Development passed a non-binding motion, 11 votes to 7, asking him to resign [5]. People Power (人民力量) reported him to the police over an alleged false declaration, and RFI reported that the police accepted the case [60].
- **Libel case.** Parents at the Chinese International School (漢基國際學校) sued Chan and his wife over emails sent in December 2011 alleging that their children had cheated. The High Court found against the couple on 30 September 2014 and awarded HK$230,000 in damages [4][5]. The Court of Appeal reversed this on 23 December 2016. The Court of Final Appeal allowed the plaintiffs' appeal on 10 April 2018 and ordered a retrial [5].
- **Driving.**
  - In October 2012 Apple Daily published footage of him driving after drinking beer. He said he believed he was within the legal limit, and the Department of Justice decided in January 2013 not to prosecute [4][5].
  - In March 2014 he pleaded guilty to careless driving over an August 2013 collision with a taxi. He was fined HK$1,800 [5].

## 7. Public finances: the numbers behind the debate

**Deficits during his term.** Hong Kong had deficits after the pandemic years:

- For 2022-23, Chan first forecast HK$56.3 billion. The outturn was about HK$140 billion [4].
- Government accounts show the following (HK$ million) [22]:

| Year | Balance before bond flows | Balance after bond issuance and repayment | Fiscal reserves at 31 March |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-24 | (171,895) | (100,205) | 734,585 |
| 2024-25 | (188,311) | (80,268) | 654,317 |
| 2025-26 | (92,861) | +11,224 | 665,541 |

The gap between the two balance columns is the source of the 「發債當收入」 ("treating borrowing as revenue") argument (Section 9).

**Earlier relief measures.** Chan is associated with large one-off handouts in earlier years:

- a HK$10,000 cash payout to adult permanent residents in the 2020-21 Budget [29];
- HK$5,000 in electronic consumption vouchers in the 2021-22 Budget [30].

**The 2025-26 Budget (26 February 2025).** This introduced a "reinforced" fiscal consolidation programme [27][28]:

- a pay freeze in 2025-26 for the executive authorities, legislature, judiciary and District Council members, including civil servants;
- a cumulative 7% cut in recurrent spending by 2027-28 compared with 2023-24, saving HK$27.3 billion;
- deletion of about 10,000 civil-service posts by April 2027.

That Budget forecast a 2025-26 consolidated deficit of HK$67 billion [28].

**What happened instead.** In February 2026 Chan reported that stamp duty and profits tax had come in nearly HK$50 billion above estimate. The Operating Account therefore returned to surplus "ahead of schedule" [10]. The February revised estimate was a HK$2.9 billion consolidated surplus [11]. On 29 April he told LegCo the preliminary figure would rise to HK$11 billion [18], and the published result was HK$11.2 billion [22].

## 8. The 2026-27 Budget (delivered 25 February 2026)

**Theme and framing**

- English title: "Driving High-quality, Inclusive Growth with Innovation and Finance" [11].
- Chinese theme: 「創科驅動　金融賦能　多元發展　關愛惠民」 [18].
- The cover was purple, described as symbolising the growing internal momentum of Hong Kong's economy amid a fast-changing external environment [14].
- Chan presented it as aligning Hong Kong with the national 15th Five-Year Plan [14][26].

**Headline numbers** [11][13][14][25]

- 2026 GDP growth forecast: 2.5% to 3.5%. 2025 actual: 3.5%.
- Underlying inflation forecast 1.7%, headline 1.8%.
- 2026-27 government revenue HK$765.2 billion and expenditure HK$843.4 billion, excluding bond flows: HK$160 billion of planned issuance and HK$59.7 billion of repayments. After those flows, a consolidated surplus of HK$22.1 billion is forecast.
- Fiscal reserves estimated at HK$679.3 billion at end-March 2027 (BBC) [14], and HK$733.7 billion by end-March 2031, equal to 17.3% of GDP or about 10 months of spending [13].
- Medium Range Forecast:
  - real growth of 3% a year from 2026-27 to 2030-31;
  - capital works averaging about HK$120 billion a year;
  - an Operating Account surplus in each of the five years;
  - land premium assumed to rise to 2% of GDP from 2027-28 [13].

**Relief measures ("派糖")**

- 100% cut in 2025/26 salaries tax, tax under personal assessment and profits tax, capped at HK$3,000 per case. This covers about 2.12 million taxpayers and about 171,000 businesses and costs about HK$5.8 billion [12].
- Rates waiver of up to HK$500 per quarter for the first two quarters of 2026-27, on both domestic and non-domestic properties [11].
- An extra month of payments for Comprehensive Social Security Assistance (CSSA, 綜援), Old Age Allowance, Old Age Living Allowance and Disability Allowance, with similar treatment for the Working Family Allowance. BBC put the total at about HK$6.5 billion [11][14].
- Higher tax allowances from 2026/27 [25]:
  - basic allowance: HK$132,000 to HK$145,000;
  - married person's allowance: HK$264,000 to HK$290,000;
  - child allowance: HK$130,000 to HK$140,000.
- No consumption vouchers. Chan said it was "not necessarily the most suitable time" because consumer sentiment had improved [16].

**Revenue and spending control**

- Ad valorem stamp duty on residential deals above HK$100 million rose from 4.25% to 6.5%. This applies to instruments signed from 26 February 2026 and affects about 0.3% of transactions, raising about HK$1 billion a year [10][12].
- The first registration tax concession for private electric cars, including the "one-for-one" replacement scheme, lapsed on 31 March 2026 [11][14].
- Recurrent spending will be cut 2% in each of 2026-27 and 2027-28, saving HK$7.8 billion and HK$15.6 billion compared with 2025-26. CSSA and statutory spending are exempt [10].
- The civil-service establishment will shrink 2% a year to about 188,000 posts. Over 10,000 posts will be deleted in the current term [10].
- The Pay Trend Survey was resumed [11]. On 16 June 2026 the Chief Executive in Council approved a 2% pay rise backdated to 1 April 2026 [23]. In January 2026 Chan had said civil-service pay had a wide knock-on effect and must be handled carefully [24].

**Financing infrastructure**

- **HK$150 billion transferred from the Exchange Fund** to the Capital Works Reserve Fund, HK$75 billion in each of 2026-27 and 2027-28, for the Northern Metropolis and other projects [10]. BBC described it as the first such transfer since 1984 [14].
  - The Chief Executive in Council approved it on 14 April 2026 under section 8 of the Exchange Fund Ordinance.
  - The government cited the Fund's record HK$330 billion investment income in 2025 and assets above HK$4.1 trillion.
  - It called the transfer "exceptional" and "not a recurring measure" [17].
  - Chan's line on it: 「放在外匯基金是做投資，放在北都做基建同樣是做投資」 ("money in the Exchange Fund is an investment; money in Northern Metropolis infrastructure is also an investment") [14][16].
- The combined borrowing ceiling of the green and infrastructure bond programmes rose from HK$700 billion to HK$900 billion [10].
- Planned bond issuance is HK$160 billion to HK$220 billion a year from 2026-27 to 2030-31, about half of it to refinance maturing debt [11].
- Government debt-to-GDP rises from 14.4% to 19.9% over the forecast period. Bond money is not to be used for recurrent spending [10].
- Capital works spending in 2026-27 is about HK$128 billion [10].
- Other funds were folded back into the government accounts (about HK$15.8 billion), and the Bond Fund surplus was to be transferred [10].

**Development measures**

- **AI:**
  - a Committee on AI+ and Industry Development Strategy chaired by the Financial Secretary;
  - the Hong Kong AI Research and Development Institute to operate from the second half of 2026;
  - the Sandy Ridge (沙嶺) data facility cluster;
  - HK$50 million for AI literacy;
  - HK$100 million for government digital transformation [11][14].
- **Northern Metropolis:** HK$10 billion each to:
  - the Loop's Hong Kong Park;
  - a new company to develop San Tin Technopole;
  - a Hung Shui Kiu Industry Park company [11].
- **Finance:**
  - tax concessions for gold trading;
  - Chinese government bond futures in Hong Kong;
  - REITs added to mutual market access;
  - consultation on T+1 settlement;
  - licensing of digital-asset dealers and custodians [11].
- **Land and housing:**
  - about 22,000 private flats of land supply in 2026-27;
  - no general commercial sites for sale;
  - public housing supply of 196,000 units over five years [11].
- **Tourism and culture:** HK$1.66 billion for the Tourism Board, and HK$200 million for a Northern Metropolis rural-tourism fund [11]. HKFP reported that the harbour light show "Symphony of Lights" would be retired and replaced by light shows at tourist spots [15].
- **Tai Po fire:**
  - HK$4 billion of public money toward the HK$6.8 billion buyout of Wang Fuk Court flats, with HK$2.8 billion from the donation-funded support fund [15];
  - HK$300 million to the Urban Renewal Authority for an enhanced "Smart Tender" (招標妥) system against bid-rigging;
  - HK$3 billion for a new building-repair subsidy scheme [11][14].

**Legislative passage**

- The public consultation for the Budget opened in mid-December 2025 [61].
- LegCo passed the **Appropriation Bill 2026** on 29 April 2026 [19][20].
- During the debate:
  - Some legislators said the Exchange Fund transfer and Northern Metropolis borrowing put future finances at risk. Chan called that "scaremongering" (危言聳聽) and said it would be irresponsible *not* to invest [19][21].
  - On the "treating borrowing as revenue" charge, he said the Budget uses cash-basis accounting, which records cash in and out, and that the government was not hiding anything [21].
  - He said the transfer was less than 4% of Exchange Fund reserves [19].
  - Junius Ho (何君堯), deputy chairman of the House Committee, did not raise his hand in the vote for a second year running. Chan said whether Ho voted was 「對我嚟講無關痛癢」 ("of no consequence to me") [20].
  - An SCMP opinion piece of 4 May 2026 was headlined "Paul Chan should welcome lawmakers' budget scrutiny, not dismiss it" [67].
- On 15 May 2026 the FSO announced a further non-recurrent HK$5 billion appropriation to the special fund for national-security spending, according to the UK government's six-monthly report [57]. An earlier HK$8 billion allocation was made in the 2021 Budget [4].

## 10. Current state of affairs (October 2026)

- **Economy.**
  - Real GDP grew 5.9% year on year in Q1 and 4.3% in Q2 2026, or 5.1% for the first half, the strongest half-year in nearly five years. Exports driven by AI-related electronics were the main factor [32].
  - The official 2026 forecast is 3.5% to 4.5% [32].
  - Unemployment was 3.9% in July-September 2026, and underlying inflation was 2.1% in September [33].
- **Policy Address and Five-Year Plan.** On 16 September 2026 John Lee delivered the Policy Address titled "A Strategic Vision for a Bright New Era, Driving Reform and Boosting Development, Unleashing Opportunities and Enhancing Livelihood". It was built on Hong Kong's First Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) [34]. The plan has five major goals [35]:
  - economic breakthroughs;
  - global competitiveness;
  - faster Northern Metropolis development;
  - better livelihoods;
  - integration into national development.

  Policy Address items in Chan's portfolio [34]:
  - tax concessions for the gold and commodities ecosystem;
  - RMB-denominated, physically settled gold futures;
  - studying a larger gold holding for the Exchange Fund;
  - 5% or half-rate tax for selected enterprises in key sectors.

  Chan answered the economic questions at the joint press conference [36].
- **Competition with Singapore.** In a Bloomberg TV interview reported on 23 September, Chan said Hong Kong would not "race to the bottom" with Singapore on tax breaks. He also said there was "no intention" to change the Hong Kong dollar's peg to the US dollar [38].
- **APEC.** Hong Kong hosted the APEC Finance Ministers' Meeting on 20-21 October 2026, chaired by China's Minister of Finance Lan Fo'an. It issued a Joint Statement. Chan spoke on financial inclusion and the Young Financial Talents Dialogue [39].
- **Next Budget.** The 2027-28 Budget will be due in early 2027. As of 22 October 2026 no consultation date had been found in official sources (unconfirmed).

## 11. Ongoing issues and debates

1. **Borrowing and the Exchange Fund.** The debate is about whether bond issuance (HK$160 billion to HK$220 billion a year) and the HK$150 billion transfer weaken the fiscal and monetary buffers. The government says bonds fund only infrastructure, debt stays below 20% of GDP, and the Exchange Fund's ability to defend the peg is not affected [10][17][19].
2. **How surpluses are reported.** Critics point to the "before bonds" deficit of HK$92.9 billion in 2025-26, against a HK$11.2 billion "after bonds" surplus [22]. Chan calls the criticism a misunderstanding of cash-basis accounting [21].
3. **Austerity versus relief.** The debate covers civil-service cuts and the end of the pay freeze, the decision not to issue consumption vouchers, and the end of the EV car tax concession [10][16][21][23].
4. **The Northern Metropolis.** Its cost, its land-premium financing and how fast it is moving [11][14][19].
5. **Tai Po fire follow-up.** Topics include the buyout, building-repair bid-rigging and the Independent Committee's work. SCMP reported in January 2026 that no cabinet reshuffle would happen until the fire inquiry ended [15][53][66].
6. **The 2027 Chief Executive race.** Election Committee subsector elections are set for 22 November 2026. Of the 1,500 members, 982 are elected, for a term from 1 February 2027 to 31 January 2032. John Lee's term ends on 30 June 2027 [49]. Nominations closed on 20 October with 1,155 nomination forms [50]. Lee has not said whether he will seek a second term [52].
   - A Macau-based commentary names Chief Secretary Eric Chan Kwok-ki (陳國基), Paul Chan and Secretary for Security Chris Tang Ping-keung (鄧炳強) as possible contenders (「雙陳，一鄧」).
   - The same commentary notes that Paul Chan would be 72 in 2027 and, unlike the other two, is not on the US sanctions list. This is commentary, not a confirmed candidacy [51].

## 13. Things easily confused

- **Paul Chan Mo-po (陳茂波)** and **Chan Kin-por (陳健波)**: the latter is a different person, a legislator. Chinese Wikipedia carries a disambiguation note [5]. **"Paul Chan"** is also a common name; English Wikipedia disambiguates [4].
- **Financial Secretary** and **Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury** (Christopher Hui, 許正宇): the FS sits above the bureau secretary. Hui runs the FSTB under the FS [3][6].
- **Financial Secretary** and **Chief Secretary**: two different Secretaries of Department, and both are currently surnamed Chan (陳茂波 and 陳國基) [6].
- **Deputy Financial Secretary** (黃偉綸) and an **Under Secretary**: the deputy post exists only at department level. An Under Secretary (e.g. Bernard Chan, Under Secretary for Commerce and Economic Development) deputises for a bureau secretary [42].
- **Budget** (February, by the FS) and **Policy Address** (施政報告; September or October, by the Chief Executive) [11][34].
- **Fiscal reserves** (HK$665.5 billion at 31 March 2026) and the **Exchange Fund** (over HK$4.1 trillion in assets) [17][22].
- **Two kinds of year label.** Fiscal years run April to March and are written "2026-27". Tax years of assessment are written "2025/26" [12].
- **Mainland titles.** The Hong Kong FS is not China's Minister of Finance (財政部部長). Mainland outlets use simplified 财政司司长 for the Hong Kong post [14][40].
- **Acting holders.** Before Chan's appointment, the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury, KC Chan (陳家強), briefly acted as Financial Secretary after John Tsang left in 2017 [5].
