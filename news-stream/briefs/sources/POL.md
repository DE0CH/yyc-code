# Hong Kong Political Gossip and Insider Political Reporting (政治花絮 / 政壇專欄): Background Brief

*Information current to 22 October 2026. Every factual statement carries a numbered citation; the source list is at the end. Statements marked "unconfirmed" or "Claude's note" are not taken from a source.*

---

## 1. What the subject is

Hong Kong newspapers and news sites run a recurring genre of political column. These columns report what is said in private about politicians and officials: who is rising or falling, who has Beijing's backing, who will be reappointed or dropped, who said what at a closed meeting or a dinner, and how legislators are behaving. Most are written under a pen name or a column title and cite unnamed sources such as "政界人士" (political figures), "建制中人" (people inside the pro-Beijing camp) or "知情人士" (informed sources) [33][39][43].

Examples current in 2026:

- **《大棋盤》("The Big Chessboard")**, Sing Tao (星島) / stheadline.com politics section. Signed with the pen name 聶風. On 6 August 2026 it reported that some legislators had "zero speeches" (零發言) in committees, that LegCo President Starry Lee shared her rebuttal article in the legislators' messaging group, and that two first-term legislators from the Election Committee constituency were drawing criticism [33]. On 24 September 2026 it reported the Liberal Party's plan to abolish its "party leader" post [60].
- **《政官莊》**, East Week (東周刊, part of the Sing Tao group). On 15 October 2026 it reported talk in political circles that someone would stand against John Lee in the 2027 Chief Executive election and was "looking for people to form a campaign team" (埋班組競選團) [43].
- **《一片破局》and《拆局》**, HK01 (香港01), 政情 section. A 17 October 2025 piece described the wave of legislators not running again, the "7字頭" (in-their-seventies) legislators, and a cancelled farewell lunch with the Chief Executive [6].
- **《有政戲》**, Hong Kong Economic Times (香港經濟日報, HKET), by 傅流螢. On 10 April 2026 it assessed the 40 first-term legislators after 100 days [37].
- **"Ray Online"**, am730. On 21 October 2026 it reported that legislator Junius Ho (何君堯) withdrew his nomination for the University of Hong Kong council seat and answered a question about whether a "高人" (someone higher up) had told him to: "好多高人，好多人高過我" [69].
- **《撥亂反正》**, Oriental Daily News (東方日報), signed "政情觀察員 黃傑士" [70].
- **【香港政情】**, a news tag used by etnet (經濟通), for example for Election Committee nomination news [50].
- Opinion columns in Sing Tao's New York edition (星島日報 美東版) also gossip about succession. A column of 21 August 2026 said "北方探子" (scouts from the north) were in Hong Kong "收風" (gathering opinions) on the government's performance. It named the two likeliest outcomes for the next Chief Executive as "超哥冧莊" (John Lee staying on) or "陳國基上" (Eric Chan taking over) [38].

Mainstream reporting covers the same ground, sometimes from the same sources. On 14 July 2026 Ming Pao reported, as relayed by Lianhe Zaobao, that Beijing's "收風人" (opinion-gatherers) had met Hong Kong political and business figures one by one. According to that report they asked about senior officials' performance "逐個問" (one by one), to collect views on the next Chief Executive and the next team [39].

## 2. Names and vocabulary

**For the genre itself:** 政治花絮 (political titbits), 政壇專欄 (political column), 政情 (political situation; also a section name at HK01 [6] and Sing Tao [33]), 拆局 ("taking the situation apart") [6], 收風 (picking up word / gathering intelligence) [38][39].

**Terms for Beijing and its offices:**
- **阿爺** ("grandpa"): colloquial for the central government, as in "阿爺會如何布陣" ("how grandpa will deploy his pieces") [38].
- **中央**: the central authorities.
- **西環** (Sai Wan, Western District): the Liaison Office, which sits in Sai Wan. "西環治港" ("Sai Wan governing Hong Kong") is a long-standing criticism of Beijing's interference [20]. The office building is 西港中心, 160 Connaught Road West [75].
- **中聯辦**: the Liaison Office; **港澳辦 / 中央港澳辦**: the Hong Kong and Macao Work Office (see section 3).
- **述職**: the Chief Executive's annual "duty report" to the state leader in Beijing [30].
- **上京**: going to Beijing. HK01 reported that a legislator in his seventies went to Beijing "陳情" (to plead his case) for renomination, without success [6].

**Terms for appointments and elections:**
- **冧莊**: a dealer keeps the bank in a card game; used for an incumbent continuing [38].
- **換馬** ("changing horses"): replacing an official, as in "高官換馬風波" (the senior-official replacement affair) [26].
- **埋班**: assembling a team [43].
- **君子賽 / 君子之爭**: a "gentlemen's contest." Starry Lee used 「君子之爭」 for her LegCo presidency race [4]; East Week used 「君子賽」 for a possible Chief Executive race [43].
- **揭盅**: lifting the dice cup, i.e. a reveal. "未到做冬，唔會揭盅" means "no reveal before the winter solstice" [38].
- **自動當選**: elected unopposed. **超額參選 / 差額選舉**: more candidates than seats [43].
- **新丁**: newcomer legislator [37]. **翻生**: coming back to life [6].

**Nicknames and labels seen in 2025–26 coverage and forums:**
- **超哥**: Chief Executive John Lee (李家超), in a newspaper column [38] and on the LIHKG forum [71].
- **強哥 / 蛋強**: Secretary for Security Chris Tang (鄧炳強), on LIHKG [71].
- **大主席** ("big chairman"): the President of the Legislative Council. Used for Andrew Leung (梁君彥) [6] and for the 2026 presidency race [5].
- **班長** ("class monitor"): the pro-Beijing camp's convenor in LegCo. HK01 used it for Martin Liao (廖長江), also called by his given name 「長江」 [6].
- **7字頭議員**: legislators aged 70 or more [6].
- **G19**: the DAB's 19-member bloc in the 2022–25 LegCo [6].
- **C15+**: a legislators' group whose convenor is 陳紹雄 [33].
- **武官治港** ("governance by uniformed officers"): describes the many former police and disciplined-services officers in top posts [28][29].

## 3. Beijing's bodies for Hong Kong

**Central Hong Kong and Macao Work Office / Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office of the State Council**
- Chinese: 中共中央港澳工作辦公室 / 國務院港澳事務辦公室. Short forms: 中央港澳辦, 港澳辦, HKMAO.
- It was reorganised in 2023 as an office of the Communist Party Central Committee, and its leadership was announced in July 2023 [14].
- Director: **Xia Baolong (夏寶龍)** [12].
- Since 25 March 2026 the leadership has been "one director, five deputies" (一正五副) [12]:
  - **Xu Qifang (徐啟方)**, deputy director in charge of daily work.
  - **Zhou Ji (周霽)**, who is also Liaison Office director.
  - **Zheng Xincong (鄭新聰)**, who is also director of the Macao Liaison Office [13].
  - **Nong Rong (農融)**.
  - **Zhang Dongmei (章冬梅)**, appointed 25 March 2026. She is a former vice-chair of the All-China Women's Federation and the office's first female leadership member [12][13].
- Former deputy **Wang Linggui (王靈桂)** left in October 2025 to become chair of the China Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese [13].
- Xia Baolong's January 2026 speech on "堅持和完善行政主導" (upholding and improving executive-led government) set five expectations for the new legislators, described below [31][32].

**Central Leading Group on Hong Kong and Macao Work (中央港澳工作領導小組)**
- The Party's senior coordinating group. Wikipedia lists Ding Xuexiang (丁薛祥), Politburo Standing Committee member and Vice-Premier, first in its membership, followed by Xia Baolong [19].

**Liaison Office of the Central People's Government in the HKSAR**
- Chinese: 中央人民政府駐香港特別行政區聯絡辦公室. Short forms: 中聯辦, LOCPG; colloquially 西環.
- Director: **Zhou Ji (周霽)**, appointed 30 May 2025. He replaced **Zheng Yanxiong (鄭雁雄)**, who had been director since January 2023 [9].
- Zhou is also a HKMAO deputy director and the National Security Adviser to Hong Kong's Committee for Safeguarding National Security. He was previously a provincial official in Hubei and Henan, and HKMAO's executive deputy director at full-minister rank [11].
- Zheng Yanxiong was reported to be moving to a vice-chairmanship of the NPC Education, Science, Culture and Public Health Committee [10].
- Deputy-level changes:
  - 1 June 2026: **Yuan Gujie (袁古潔)**, previously a Guangdong Party Standing Committee member and political-legal affairs secretary, became a deputy director, replacing **Liu Guangyuan (劉光源)**, a former Foreign Ministry commissioner in Hong Kong. **Liu Songlin (劉松林)** became secretary-general [16][76].
- Restructuring, made public on 2 July 2026 [15]:
  - The office went from 24 bodies to 23.
  - The Economic Department became two "Economic and Financial" departments (經濟金融一部、二部).
  - The Education, Science and Technology Department and the Youth Work Department merged into an Education and Youth Department (教育和青年部).
  - The district work departments became Hong Kong Island and Kowloon "affairs departments," and the New Territories department was split into New Territories East and New Territories West.
  - A Social Development Department (社會發展部) was added.
  - HKET reported that the reorganisation had largely been completed early in 2026 [15].

**Other central bodies posts often mention**
- **Office for Safeguarding National Security of the CPG in the HKSAR (駐港國家安全公署, 國安公署):** head **Dong Jingwei (董經緯)** [17]; deputy head **Sun Qingye (孫青野)** [18].
- **Foreign Ministry Commissioner's Office (外交部駐港公署):** commissioner **Cui Jianchun (崔建春)** [17].
- **PLA Hong Kong Garrison (解放軍駐港部隊):** political commissar **Lai Ruxin (賴如鑫)** [17].
- These are separate bodies from the Liaison Office.

## 5. The Legislative Council after the December 2025 election

**Structure**
- 90 seats: 20 directly elected geographical constituency (GC) seats, 40 seats chosen by the Election Committee (選委界), and 30 functional constituency (FC, 功能界別) seats [1].
- The 8th LegCo's term began on 1 January 2026 and lasts four years [1][8].

**The election (7 December 2025)**
- Turnout was 31.90% (1,317,682 voters), against 30.2% in 2021. Registered GC voters numbered 4,131,298 [1][2].
- Seats won by party: DAB 20, Business and Professionals Alliance (BPA) 8, FTU 7, Liberal Party 4, New People's Party 3, Federation of Hong Kong and Kowloon Labour Unions 2, Federation of Education Workers 2, Professional Power 1, Roundtable 1 [1].
- Campaigning was suspended after the Wang Fuk Court fire of 26 November 2025, but the vote went ahead [1].
- The government spent HK$1.28 billion on publicity [1].
- 161 people were nominated, 107 of them newcomers [1].
- Third Side (新思維), the only self-described moderate party, lost its only seat when its candidate failed to obtain enough nominations [1].

**The retirement wave**
- 35 incumbents did not run again, including all 12 who were over 70 [1]. Among them:
  - President **Andrew Leung (梁君彥)**, who announced on 29 September 2025 [1]
  - Regina Ip, Tommy Cheung, Lo Wai-kwok (盧偉國), Michael Tien (田北辰), Jeffrey Lam, Martin Liao, Chan Kin-por, Tik Chi-yuen (狄志遠) and Doreen Kong (江玉歡) [1]
  - Paul Tse (謝偉俊), who did not file a nomination [1]
- Media reported an unwritten age limit of 70. Officials denied "blessing" (祝福) any candidate [1].
- HK01 reported that legislators who were not returning had been "notified" (接獲通知) the week before, and that a legislator in his seventies went to Beijing to plead his case without success [6].

**Leadership**
- President: **Starry Lee Wai-king (李慧琼)**, DAB, Kowloon Central, a member of the NPC Standing Committee. On 8 January 2026 she beat **Ronick Chan Chun-ying (陳振英)**, Finance FC and an NPC deputy, by 47 votes to 42 [3][4].
- On 12 January 2026 the following were elected unopposed [7]:
  - House Committee: chair Ronick Chan, deputy Junius Ho (何君堯)
  - Finance Committee: chair **Jimmy Ng Wing-ka (吳永嘉)**, BPA leader; deputy Gary Chan (陳克勤)

**Conduct and performance stories in 2026**
- **Oath-taking, 1 January 2026:** seven members were told to repeat their oaths, and Junius Ho stumbled over his text [8].
- **Xia Baolong's speech, 26 January 2026:** he asked legislators to "支持政府不缺位、監督政府不越位" (support the government without absence, oversee it without overstepping), to put LegCo work first, to mind their personal conduct, and for no one to "掉隊" (drop out) midway [31][32].
- **Directorship declarations:** the SCMP found that several new members had not declared unpaid directorships. Named were Andrew Yao (姚祖輝) and Lothair Lam (林鳴鋒) [73].
- **Voting-presence rate:** LegCo began publishing a "投票在席率" (presence at votes). After 100 days, 19 members were below 100% [74].
- **Newcomers' activity:** SCMP found newcomers asked fewer questions but attended more. Those named included Alan Chan (陳宗彝) of Miramar Group and fencer-turned-legislator **Vivian Kong (江旻憓)** [35].
- **"Zero speeches" row:** Ming Pao reported that at least 17 members had never spoken at some committees. Starry Lee replied in a newspaper article [34]. Sing Tao's 《大棋盤》 described members praising "主席英明" ("the wise President") after she shared that article [33].
- **Beijing study visit:** 89 legislators went on a week-long national-affairs study visit to Beijing in July 2026 [36].

## 8. The Wang Fuk Court fire and accountability

- The fire broke out at **Wang Fuk Court (宏福苑)**, a subsidised housing estate in Tai Po, on 26 November 2025, during large-scale renovation. It engulfed seven blocks, killed 168 people and displaced more than 4,600 residents [53].
- John Lee announced an Independent Committee on 12 December 2025, and it started work on 19 December [54]. It is chaired by Mr Justice David Lok, with members Chan Kin-por and Dr Rex Auyeung [55].
- It held 30 days of evidential hearings with 80 witnesses and submitted its report on 22 October 2026 [54].
- The SCMP and TVB, citing sources, reported that the report names four government bodies. The Housing Bureau's Independent Checking Unit is said to bear the greatest responsibility; the Fire Services Department and the Buildings Department are among the others [56].
- The government said it would "shortly announce the plans for the comprehensive reforms and pursuing accountability" [53].
- CNA, citing HKET (21 September 2026), reported that political and business figures expect John Lee's reappointment to become clear around year-end. According to that report, how he handles the fire's aftermath and accountability is seen as a test [40].

## 10. Succession and the 2026–27 political calendar

**Rules for the Chief Executive election, as summarised in Sing Tao (22 October 2026)** [44]
- The Election Committee has 1,500 members in five sectors of 300 each.
- A candidate needs at least 188 nominations, including at least 15 from each sector, and more than 750 votes to win.
- If no one wins in the first round, the top two go to a second round.
- The State Council then appoints the winner.

## 11. Things that are easily confused

- **港澳辦 vs 中聯辦.** The first is the Beijing office (HKMAO), headed by Xia Baolong. The second is the Liaison Office in Hong Kong, headed by Zhou Ji. Zhou also holds a HKMAO deputy post [11][12].
- **國務院港澳辦 vs 中央港澳辦.** These are two names for the same office. The Party name dates from the 2023 reform [14].
- **Two different Zhengs.**
  - **Zheng Yanxiong (鄭雁雄):** former Liaison Office director, removed in May 2025 [9].
  - **Zheng Xincong (鄭新聰):** HKMAO deputy and director of the Macao Liaison Office [13].
- **Two different "陳" with financial roles.**
  - **Paul Chan Mo-po (陳茂波):** Financial Secretary [21].
  - **Ronick Chan Chun-ying (陳振英):** legislator and House Committee chair [7].
- **Two Chans in the Finance Committee and ExCo.** Gary Chan (陳克勤) is DAB chairman and Finance Committee deputy chair. Chan Kin-por (陳健波) is a former legislator, an ExCo member and a member of the fire inquiry [7][21][55].
- **Two Ngs (吳).** Stanley Ng Chau-pei (吳秋北) is FTU president [61]. Jimmy Ng Wing-ka (吳永嘉) is BPA party leader [58].
- **Eric Chan vs Eric (Erick) Tsang.** Eric Chan Kwok-ki (陳國基) is Chief Secretary [21]. Erick Tsang Kwok-wai (曾國衞) is the Constitutional and Mainland Affairs Secretary removed in January 2026 [23].
- **"大主席."** It means the LegCo President, not a party chair [5].
- **The two elections of late 2026 and early 2027.** The 22 November 2026 election chooses Election Committee members. It does not choose the Chief Executive; the Chief Executive is chosen by that committee in March 2027 [44][46].
- **Liaison Office vs Office for Safeguarding National Security.** They are separate central bodies with separate heads (Zhou Ji; Dong Jingwei) [11][17].
