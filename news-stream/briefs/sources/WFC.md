# Wang Fuk Court (宏福苑) and the Tai Po fire of 26 November 2025: background brief

*Status as of 22 October 2026. All times are Hong Kong Time (HKT, UTC+8).*

---

## 1. What this is about (summary)

Wang Fuk Court (宏福苑) is a subsidised housing estate in Tai Po (大埔), in Hong Kong's New Territories. It has eight 31-storey residential blocks and was built in 1983 [1][2]. On the afternoon of Wednesday 26 November 2025 a fire broke out at the estate, which was then in the middle of a HK$330 million exterior renovation. All eight blocks were wrapped in bamboo scaffolding, protective netting and foam boards over the windows [1][2]. Within hours the fire had spread to seven of the eight blocks. It was largely put out at 10:18 on 28 November, more than 43 hours after it began [2].

The final official death toll is **168**, including one firefighter [3][4]. Seventy-nine people were injured [1]. It is the deadliest fire in Hong Kong in decades: news organisations describe it as the deadliest since 1948 [1][3][10]. About 4,600 residents, roughly 1,984 households, lost their homes [2][3][26].

After the fire came:

- criminal charges for manslaughter, fraud, money laundering and other offences against the renovation consultant, the main contractor and several individuals [8][12];
- a judge-led **Independent Committee** inquiry, which held hearings from March to July 2026 and handed its report to the Chief Executive **today, 22 October 2026** [16][17];
- a government buyout of the owners' flats and a special scheme for owners to buy new subsidised flats [22][23];
- city-wide reforms, including a ban on smoking at construction sites [43][45];
- national-security and sedition arrests of people who criticised the official response [1][38].

## 3. Background

### 3.1 The estate

- Wang Fuk Court is a **Home Ownership Scheme (居屋, HOS)** court. Under HOS the government sells flats below market price, so it is subsidised ownership, not public rental [1][2].
- It was completed in 1983 and has eight 31-storey blocks with about 1,984 flats [2][36].
- The 2021 census counted 4,643 residents, almost 40% of them aged 65 or over [1][3].
- Owners whose flats had a "premium unpaid" could resell only under subsidised-housing rules. This distinction set the two buyout prices in 2026 [22].

### 3.2 The renovation project (2016 – November 2025)

- **June 2016:** the estate received a statutory notice under the **Mandatory Building Inspection Scheme (強制驗樓計劃)**, which applies to buildings aged 30 or more [2][8].
- **January 2019:** **Will Power Architects Company Limited (鴻毅建築師有限公司)** was appointed to inspect. In December 2021 it was appointed again, this time to supervise repairs [2].
- **Early 2023:** Will Power proposed three options. **January 2024:** a meeting of owners and proxy holders, representing about 30% of shares, chose the most extensive option and **Prestige Construction & Engineering Co., Limited (宏業建築工程有限公司)** as main contractor. The cost rose to HK$330 million, or HK$160,000–180,000 per household [2][36].
- **July 2024:** work began. Prestige told residents that foam boards would cover the windows [2].
- **2024–2025:** residents complained to the Labour Department, Fire Services Department (FSD), ICU, ICAC, Urban Renewal Authority and Housing Department about smoking workers, netting and foam boards. The Labour Department inspected 16 times, the last on 20 November 2025, and replied that the netting met standards [1][2][5].
- **September 2024:** owners voted out the old OC committee (chair **Tang Kwok-kuen 鄧國權**) and elected a new one (chair **Tony Tsui 徐滿柑**). The contract with Prestige had already been signed [34][36].
- **2021–2024:** **Peggy Wong (黃碧嬌)**, a DAB district councillor, was a consultant to the OC committee [5][47].
- According to ICAC Commissioner Danny Woo (胡英明), after a typhoon in July 2025 those involved bought 2,300 rolls of netting that did not meet fire-retardant standards, at HK$54 a roll, enough to wrap all eight blocks. After a scaffold-netting fire in Central in October 2025, they bought 115 compliant rolls at HK$100 a roll and fitted them at the base of each block, where inspectors take samples [36][1].

### 3.3 Earlier scaffold fires in 2025

Before November 2025 there were several fires involving bamboo scaffolding and netting: Texaco Road (Tsuen Wan) in February, Chinachem Tower (華懋大廈, Central) in October, and construction sites in Kai Tak and at the University of Hong Kong in October [1]. In March 2025 the government had announced a phase-out of bamboo scaffolding in favour of metal [1].

## 4. The fire: timeline, 26–28 November 2025

- **About 14:43, 26 Nov:** the fire starts on a flat roof in the first-floor light well of **Wang Cheong House (Block F)**, outside flats 104 and 105. Officials say the likely cause is a lit cigarette igniting cardboard boxes [5][6]. Within about 15 minutes it reaches the roof [6].
- **14:51:** first call to the FSD. Fire crews arrive about 14:56–15:02 [2][6].
- **15:02:** No. 3 alarm. **15:34:** No. 4. **18:22:** **No. 5**, the highest. This was the first No. 5 alarm since the 2008 Cornwall Court fire (嘉禾大廈) [1][2].
- The fire climbs the netting and scaffolding, gets in through windows covered with foam boards, and spreads to six more blocks. Only Wang Chi House (H) escapes [1].
- **The fire alarms do not sound in any block.** Residents are warned by neighbours knocking on doors and by chat groups [1][5].
- **Firefighter Ho Wai-ho (何偉豪), 37,** of Sha Tin Fire Station, is found with burns at 16:01 and declared dead at 16:45 [2][52]. The committee later found that he most likely fell from the 31st floor [1].
- **27 Nov:** Chief Executive John Lee holds an early-morning press conference: 36 dead, 279 missing [2]. Police arrest three men from Prestige on suspicion of manslaughter [2].
- **28 Nov, 10:18:** the FSD announces the fire is largely put out. About 2,311 firefighters took part [1][2]. The ICAC arrests eight people [37].
- **29 Nov – 1 Dec:** three days of official mourning, with flags at half-mast [1][2].

### Casualties

- The death toll was revised several times (44 → 128 → 151 → 159 → 160 → 161) before reaching **168** on **15 January 2026**, when Secretary for Security Chris Tang (鄧炳強) said all remains had been identified [3][4][2].
- The dead were 58 males and 110 females, aged **6 months to 98 years**. They include 10 domestic workers (nine Indonesian, one Filipina), five construction workers and two interior decorators [3][4].
- 164 were found at the scene and four died in hospital [3].
- 91 died from inhaling hot smoke, according to the inquiry's experts [6].
- The government did not publish the names [4]. A list of all 168 names was later attached to the June 2026 charge sheets [9].

## 5. Causes as established so far

From official statements, charge sheets and evidence at the inquiry [5][6][8][12][44]:

1. **Ignition:** most likely smoking. The government laboratory and the inter-departmental Fire Investigation Task Force ruled out other causes [5][6].
2. **Netting:** much of the protective netting was not fire-retardant. Seven of 20 police samples failed tests, and most stored samples failed government laboratory tests [2][6]. Compliant netting had been placed only at the base of the scaffolding, where samples are taken [1][36].
3. **Foam boards over windows:** they let the fire into flats and blocked residents' view of the fire [5][6].
4. **Staircase windows removed:** fire-resistant windows in escape staircases were replaced with wooden boards or openings so workers could reach the scaffolding. In simulation tests, staircase temperatures went above 600 °C with the openings, against 56 °C with the windows in place [6][8][13].
5. **Fire alarms and hoses disabled:** an electrician employed by the estate manager **ISS EastPoint Properties (置邦)** testified that he turned off main switches in all eight blocks, as instructed through the management company at Prestige's request, so that water tanks could be emptied. This also cut power to the alarms. A fire-safety contractor, **China Status**, had filed 85 shutdown notices with the FSD for the hydrant and hose-reel system between April and September 2025 without visiting the site. Another, **Victory Fire Engineering**, found on 19 November that the switches were off and did not follow it up [5].
6. **Weather:** dry northeast monsoon conditions, and a Red Fire Danger Warning in force [1].

## 8. Rehousing and the government buyout

**Immediately after the fire**, residents were housed in shelters, hotels, youth hostels, transitional housing and Housing Society flats [1]. Rent was waived for tenants until 31 May 2026, according to the government's fire-support website [57].

**The long-term plan (21 February 2026)** [22][23]:

- The ICU assessed that Blocks A–G were in no immediate danger of collapse but could not reasonably be restored, and would **all be demolished**. That covers 1,736 flats.
- The government offered to buy every flat in A–G at **HK$8,000 per sq ft** of saleable area if the land premium was unpaid, or **HK$10,500 per sq ft** if paid. The government said this would not set a precedent.
- The buyer is **Wang Fuk Court Property Rights Acquisition Limited**, a company wholly owned through Financial Secretary Incorporated [23].
- Owners can take cash or a **"Flat-for-Flat" voucher**. Either can be used in the **Special Sales Exercise (特設銷售計劃)** for reserved new HOS, Green Form Subsidised Home Ownership Scheme (GSH) and Housing Society flats. Projects include 盛緻苑 (Kowloon Bay), 啟陽苑 (Kai Tak), 匯熙苑 (Kam Tin), 裕豐苑 (Tung Chung), 朗風苑 (Ping Shan), 影輝苑 (Tseung Kwan O), and Housing Society projects at Pak Wo Road (Fanling) and Anderson Road Quarry (Kwun Tong). A new HOS project of about 1,500 flats at **Chung Nga Road West (頌雅路西), Tai Po**, is due from 2029 for those who want to stay in Tai Po [22][24].
- The 2026–27 Budget set aside HK$4 billion of public money for Blocks A–G. Buying Wang Chi House was estimated at about HK$1 billion more [24].

**Wang Chi House (Block H, 宏志閣)** was not burned. The government first said it would include the block only if owners reached a "high degree of consensus" [22]. On 26 June, 193 owners (77.8%) had signed acceptance letters, so the plan was opened to them on condition that at least 75% sign sale agreements by 15 October [24]. By 2 September, 188 owners (about 75.8%) had signed, and the government confirmed it would proceed with buying the block [27].

**Uptake:**

- Buyout deadline, 31 August: 1,971 owners (99.3%) had returned acceptance letters [27][29]. Thirteen did not [26].
- About 1,700 households chose the Special Sales Exercise [28].
- Sale-agreement deadline, 15 October: 1,850 owners (93.2%) had signed. The others could not sign in time because of probate, bankruptcy, title disputes, mental incapacity or absence from Hong Kong, and are being handled case by case [31].
- The government has said it could legislate to acquire unsold titles as early as next year, at a compensation "substantially lower" than the buyout price [26].
- Flat selection began on 29 September at the Housing Authority sales office in Kwun Tong and is expected to end in late November [28][29]. Many buyers have to top up the price from savings or pay stamp duty, and some have asked for relief [29][30].

## 9. Criminal cases

**Arrests:** by June 2026, police and the ICAC's joint task force had arrested 35 people in total [8]. The ICAC separately arrested people including OC committee members [10].

**10 June 2026 charges** (West Kowloon Magistrates' Courts, cases WKCC 2701 and 2702/2026) [8][56][11]:

- **Manslaughter (5 counts):** Ho Kin-yip, Ng Yeuk, Wong Hap-yin, Prestige and Will Power, for gross negligence in supervising materials and procedures: non-fire-retardant nets and canvas, flammable foam boards, and openings in staircase fire escapes [8]. The three men were refused bail [9].
- **ICAC (20 counts):** three counts of conspiracy to defraud, five of money laundering, two of attempting to pervert the course of justice, and ten tax-related counts [8]. The allegations are:
  - **Tender fraud (May 2023 – April 2024):** hiding Prestige's eight-year litigation record and inflating its tender score so that owners would vote for it; and publishing a false tender report on the Urban Renewal Authority's platform.
  - **Supervision fraud:** across 86 Will Power projects, falsely claiming that Registered Inspector Ng Yeuk carried out his statutory duties.
  - **Money laundering:** more than HK$40 million in cash handled by Wong Hap-yin, his wife Chung So-fan and Li Min, 2019–2025.
  - **Perverting justice:** on 27 November 2025, hiding a bag with HK$600,000 in cash (suspected to be part of bribes from Prestige to Wong) and inciting Ng to make false statements to the ICU [8].
- **2 September:** prosecutors asked for more time for expert evidence and new ICAC evidence. Will Power had no legal representation. The case was adjourned to **29 October 2026** [11].

**9 September 2026:** the Buildings Department laid **259 charges** under the Buildings Ordinance against Prestige, Will Power, Wong Hap-yin, Ng Yeuk, Hau Wa-kin and Ho Kin-yip. The Labour Department brought **25 prosecutions** under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance over the deaths of five construction workers [12][13].

**Related cases:**

- Jason Kong and his wife (pandemic loan fraud): District Court mention on 27 October [30].
- Three workers charged with stealing HK$90,000 of jewellery from a fire-hit flat [53].
- A man charged with flying a drone in the restricted zone over the estate; next hearing 25 November [30].
- A disciplinary board hearing against the fire-safety firms was postponed in September [53].
- Creditors have petitioned the High Court to wind up Prestige [30].

## 10. The Independent Committee and today's report

- **Status:** a non-statutory "Independent Committee", **not** a statutory Commission of Inquiry (調查委員會) under Cap. 86. Its terms of reference let it ask to be converted [14]. The chair decided not to ask in June 2026 [7].
- **Terms of reference:** causes and spread of the fire; fire service installations; construction safety; materials and testing; the roles of officials, professionals and contractors; systemic problems such as connected interests, collusion and bid-rigging; whether laws and penalties are adequate; and recommendations. Criminal liability is outside its scope [14].
- **Process:** 30 hearing days, 80 witnesses, more than 1 million files (over 2 TB) [16].
- **Report submitted 22 October 2026** [16][17]. Lee said the government will "shortly announce the plans for the comprehensive reforms and pursuing accountability". The report will be published after legal advice on not prejudicing the criminal trials [17].
- RTHK says the report runs to hundreds of pages in more than 10 chapters, in Chinese and English [18]. SCMP says it is two volumes [19].
- According to **unnamed sources** cited by SCMP and The Standard (not yet officially confirmed), the report:
  - singles out the **ICU, Buildings Department, FSD and Labour Department**, with the ICU bearing the heaviest responsibility;
  - does not name individual officials;
  - recommends criminal penalties for bid-rigging, tighter supervision of professionals, clearer material standards and changes to fire-certificate procedures [19][20].
- A Bloomberg preview (21 October) said the report would put government accountability in focus [54].

## 11. Reforms announced

- **Construction-site smoking ban** in force since 17 July 2026 [43].
- **2026 Policy Address (16 September)** [45][46]:
  - amendments to the Fire Services Ordinance (a "responsible persons" regime, higher penalties);
  - amendments to the Buildings Ordinance (tighter control of major maintenance, heavier sanctions);
  - a proposal to **criminalise bid-rigging** in major maintenance;
  - Building Management Ordinance changes on quorums, proxies and declarations of interest;
  - government tender-invitation and evaluation services, and "pre-qualified lists" of vetted consultants and contractors;
  - FSD's IoT fire-detection system extended to old buildings of seven storeys or more.

  Most bills are due at LegCo "this year".
- Labour Department guidelines on toe-board materials [42].
- On 6 October Lee did not say whether the civil service's "Tier II" accountability investigation mechanism had been activated [21].

## 12. Ongoing issues and debates

- **Who is responsible:** the contractors and professionals, which is the government's position [44]; or the regulators as well, which was committee counsel's position and is the residents' view [21][44].
- **Inquiry format:** a non-statutory committee rather than a statutory Commission of Inquiry with power to compel witnesses [7][34].
- **Whistle-blowing before the fire:** complaints to several departments and the ICAC before the fire did not lead to action [1][2][5].
- **Proxy votes and bid-rigging** in owners' corporations. Complaints about corruption in building renovation reportedly rose 150% after the fire [53][47].
- **Buyout terms:** the price has been described as generous [32]. A minority refused to sell, citing loss of property and community, and some want rebuilding on the original site [32][26]. There are disputes over the insurance of about HK$2 billion held by the OC, which industry sources say may only be used for reinstatement [33]. The government has published rebuttals of what it calls rumours [57].
- **Speech and policing:** sedition and national-security arrests, the expulsion of Miles Kwan, the suspension of the Hong Kong Baptist University student union, and warnings to foreign media [1][38][39]. A columnist, Wong Kwok-ngon, was also arrested [10].
- **Victims' names:** the government did not publish the list; it later appeared with the charges [4][9].
- **Bamboo scaffolding** versus metal scaffolding [1].

## 13. Related events and easily confused items

- **Kwong Fuk Estate (廣福邨):** the neighbouring public-rental estate. It was not burned [51].
- **Cornwall Court fire (嘉禾大廈, 2008):** the previous No. 5 alarm fire [1].
- **Garley Building fire (嘉利大廈, 1996):** the previous worst peacetime building fire in Hong Kong [1].
- **Wing On warehouse fire (1948, Shek Tong Tsui):** the comparison used in reports calling this the deadliest fire since 1948 [1].
- **Grenfell Tower fire (London, 2017):** a frequent comparison. Grenfell United sent condolences [1].
- **2018 Tai Po Road bus accident:** its "independent review committee" was the model for this committee. The name is similar but it is a different Tai Po event [1].
- **"Independent Committee" vs "Commission of Inquiry":** different legal status (section 10) [14].
- **Chinachem Tower fire (Central, October 2025)** and other scaffold fires in 2025 [1].
- **The 7 December 2025 LegCo election**, held during the aftermath [40].
- **"Tai Po fire" can also mean other fires in Tai Po.** In 2025–26 it nearly always means this one.
