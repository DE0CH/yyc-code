# Galbot (銀河通用): background brief

*Compiled 22 October 2026. Every factual statement carries a numbered citation; the source list is at the end. Figures the company gives about itself (store counts, order counts, "world first" claims) are reported as the company's own statements. Where sources disagree, both versions are given.*

## 1. What the subject is

Galbot is a Beijing-based company that builds general-purpose robots and the AI models that control them. It describes itself as a "general-purpose robotics company centred on embodied foundation models" founded in 2023 [1]. It was registered on 19 May 2023 in Haidian District, Beijing [2]. It sells wheeled two-armed robots (Galbot G1, Galbot S1), a small walking humanoid (Galbot ET1), robot-staffed mini shops called 銀河太空艙 / Galbot Store, and robot-run pharmacy order-picking systems [1, 3, 4].

"Embodied AI" (具身智能) is the Chinese industry term for AI that controls a physical body such as a robot; Galbot is one of the most heavily funded companies in that sector in China. The company says its cumulative funding is the largest of any Chinese embodied-AI company and that it is the highest-valued unlisted humanoid-robot company [5]. Reported total funding is about RMB 7 billion between June 2023 and March 2026 [6], and its valuation was reported at over USD 3 billion (over RMB 20 billion) after its December 2025 round [7].

In Hong Kong the company is known mainly for three things: the Hong Kong government-owned investment fund 港投公司 (HKIC) invested in it in 2024 [8]; it opened Hong Kong's first robot-staffed retail kiosks on 1 September 2026 [9]; and it is widely reported to be preparing a Hong Kong stock-market listing, which the company has not confirmed [10, 11].

## 2. Names and terms

**The company**

- Legal name (Chinese): 北京銀河通用機器人股份有限公司 (Simplified: 北京银河通用机器人股份有限公司). Until November 2025 it was 北京銀河通用機器人有限公司; the change to a 股份有限公司 (company limited by shares) took effect on 27 November 2025 [12, 2].
- English names in use: Galbot; Beijing Galbot Co., Ltd. (used by Bloomberg and HK government press material) [13, 14]; Galbot Co. Ltd. (HKIC press release) [8]; Galaxy General Robot Co. (The Robot Report) [15]. The registry site Tianyancha machine-translates the name as "Beijing Galaxy General Robotics Co., Ltd." [2].
- Short Chinese forms: 銀河通用 / 银河通用; 銀河通用機器人. Hong Kong media usually write 機械人 ("machine-person") for "robot" where mainland media write 機器人 / 机器人, so Hong Kong headlines read 銀河通用機械人 [16, 17].
- Subsidiaries named in sources: 深圳銀河通用具身智能機器人有限公司 (Shenzhen) [18]; Suzhou Galbot Technology Co Ltd in Suzhou Industrial Park [19]; 博銀合創, a 50/50 joint venture with Bosch's investment arm 博原資本 (Boyuan Capital) [20]. Tianyancha lists a corporate group headed "海南銀河通用機器人" with 33 companies [2].

**Products, models and mascots**

- **Galbot G1**: wheeled robot with two arms and a liftable torso; the robot shown on the 2026 CCTV Spring Festival Gala and used in the stores [21, 22].
- **Galbot S1**: heavy-load industrial version, launched 5 January 2026 [21].
- **Galbot ET1 / 銀河星仔** ("Galaxy little star"): the first walking (bipedal) humanoid, unveiled August 2026 [3, 23].
- **小蓋 (Xiao Gai)**: nickname of the G1 robot as a character. The robot introduces itself as 蓋伯特 / 蓋博特 ("Gai-bo-te", a transliteration of Galbot) [24, 25]. In the Hong Kong stores 小蓋 is the robot "store manager" (店長) [26]. In the 2026 Spring Festival Gala sketch it was called 沈騰's "鐵哥們" ("iron buddy") [27].
- **銀河星腦 / AstraBrain**: the company's name for its robot-control model family. Sub-models: AstraBrain-WAM (a "world-action model"), AstraBrain-WBC (whole-body motion control), AstraBrain-Dex (dexterous hands), AstraBrain-Agent [28, 3].
- **銀河星數 / AstraData**: its training-data platform [1]; **銀河星坊 / AstraSynth**: its synthetic-data infrastructure [29].
- Research models published by the team: **GraspVLA**, **TrackVLA**, **NavFoM**, **LATENT**, **PASSAGE**, GroceryVLA, DexNDM [30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36].
- **銀河太空艙 / 银河太空舱 ("Galaxy space capsule")**: the robot-staffed kiosk shop format, about 9 m², called **Galbot Store** in Hong Kong [37, 16].

**Common context terms in posts**

- 具身智能 (embodied AI); 人形機器人 (humanoid robot); 具身大模型 (embodied foundation model); VLA (vision-language-action model).
- 遙操 / 遥操 (teleoperation, a human remotely driving the robot) versus 全自主 (fully autonomous). Whether a demonstration was teleoperated is a recurring question in coverage; Galbot stresses "零遙操、全自主" [21, 38].
- 數採中心 / 数采中心 ("data-collection centres"): government- or state-funded facilities that buy robots to gather training data; central to the 2026 revenue dispute (section 9) [39].
- 港投公司 (HKIC, Hong Kong Investment Corporation), nicknamed 「港版淡馬錫」 ("Hong Kong's Temasek") in mainland media [40].
- 寧王 / 宁王 ("King Ning"): a nickname for CATL (寧德時代), used in headlines such as 「萬億寧王選了銀河通用」 [41].
- 財爺 ("Uncle Finance"): Hong Kong colloquial nickname for the Financial Secretary, used in headlines about Paul Chan buying the first item at the Galbot Store [42].
- 18C: Chapter 18C of the HKEX Listing Rules for specialist technology companies, cited in the 2026 allegations about Galbot's listing plans [43].

## 5. Robots and models

**Galbot G1.** A wheeled robot with two arms on a torso that rises and lowers. Galbot's data sheet as summarised by Vuntum: height change 650 mm, arm length 710 mm, 5 kg per arm (10 kg total), 21 degrees of freedom, choice of hand, suction cup or gripper, four-wheel omnidirectional base up to 1.5 m/s, 8 hours per charge, height 1.73 m, weight 92.5 kg [69]. The early G1 had a suction cup on one arm and a gripper on the other [48]; Hong Kong reporters observed the same layout in the stores in 2026 [70]. A "G1 Premium" version uses NVIDIA's Jetson Thor computer (shown August 2025) [71]. JD.com listed the Spring Festival Gala G1 at about RMB 630,000 (RMB 699,900 before subsidy) [27]. Huxiu reported over 1,200 G1 units shipped in 2025 and over 1,100 in the first half of 2026 [63].

**Galbot S1.** Heavy-load industrial robot launched 5 January 2026: two arms carrying up to 50 kg together (32 kg with arms extended), vision-only positioning without QR codes or reflectors, 360° obstacle avoidance, dual batteries, 8-hour runtime and battery swapping [21]. It runs on CATL battery cells [72]. At the 2026 Spring Festival Gala an S1 appeared in the 《山海尋夢》 segment [35].

**Galbot ET1 (銀河星仔).** First bipedal humanoid, launched at the World Robot Conference in Beijing in August 2026 [3, 73]. Manufacturer figures: 1.25 m tall, 32 kg, 26 degrees of freedom; described as "a humanoid robot for entertainment and performance" [74]. QbitAI reported three versions at RMB 79,000, 139,000 and 179,000 [23].

**Models.**
- **GraspVLA** (2025): a grasping model pre-trained entirely on a synthetic dataset of one billion frames (SynGrasp-1B); authors from Galbot, Peking University, the University of Hong Kong and BAAI [30].
- **TrackVLA** (1 June 2025): a navigation/person-following model trained on simulated data, demonstrated on a Unitree robot dog [31, 75].
- **NavFoM** (November 2025): a navigation foundation model built with Peking University, the University of Adelaide and Zhejiang University, intended for dogs, wheeled and legged humanoids, drones and cars [32].
- **AstraBrain WAM 0.5** (mid-2026): Galbot says it unifies VLA and world-model approaches; **AstraBrain-WBC 0.5**: whole-body control trained on 20,000 hours of human motion-capture data [76].
- **LATENT**: tennis-skill learning from imperfect human motion data (arXiv March 2026) [33]; Galbot says a paper with partners received a best-paper award at IROS 2026 in Pittsburgh on 30 September 2026 [77].
- **PASSAGE** (shared 13 October 2026): a method for a humanoid to step over, duck under or turn sideways past obstacles, tested on a Unitree G1 [34].

**The synthetic-data approach.** Wang He argues that synthetic simulation data, not human-teleoperated data, is what allows scale [47]. Galbot describes its training as "mainly synthetic simulation data, supplemented by real robot data" [29]. A Suzhou government article said 90% of its training data is simulated [78]. Huxiu describes this as one side of an industry debate, citing 高季陽 of 星海圖 (Galaxea) as arguing for real data first [63].

## 6. Retail stores and pharmacies (mainland China)

**銀河太空艙 (Galbot space capsule).** A kiosk of about 9 m² in which a G1 robot greets customers, takes orders and payment, picks items and hands them over; it sells drinks, snacks, souvenirs and some medicines [37]. The first opened in August 2025 at the 中關村大融城 mall in Haidian, Beijing, with a 「十城百店」 ("ten cities, hundred stores") partner plan [37]. A Summer Palace (頤和園) unit opened on 12 September 2025 [79]. Later sites include 圓明園, the Badaling Great Wall and 鳴沙山 in Dunhuang [37].

Store counts given by the company and in coverage:
- Over 100 capsules in over 20 cities (February–March 2026) [80, 35].
- Over 170 in over 40 cities (July–August 2026) [50, 3].
- About 200 in nearly 50 cities (late August–October 2026), the figure Paul Chan used in Hong Kong [56, 37].

Galbot says single units exceeded 500 orders and RMB 10,000 of sales a day during the 2026 Lunar New Year [80]. Ifeng reported a bottle of cola at RMB 5, RMB 1.5 above nearby shops [79]. In October 2026 a G1 worked from a mobile sales van in the 陶溪川 district of Jingdezhen [37].

**Robot pharmacies (智慧藥房).** Meituan's pharmacy business (美團買藥) and Galbot showed a humanoid-robot pharmacy at the September 2024 CIFTIS trade fair and signed a strategic agreement [81]. The robots pick online orders in a back-room "instant retail" warehouse and place them in a pickup locker for delivery riders [4]. The first such pharmacy went into use in September 2024 according to Galbot [38] (Economic Daily says early 2025 [4]). In March 2026 the Haidian market regulator issued what was reported as China's first drug-retail licence (藥品經營許可證) for a humanoid-robot retail scenario, at a 海王星辰 (Neptunus) pharmacy on 丹棱街 [82, 4]. The pharmacy chain said peak daily volume was 370 orders and staffing per store fell by more than half [82]. In September 2026 the system began regular operation at a 老百姓大藥房 (LBX Pharmacy) store on 虹漕路, Xuhui, Shanghai, at 400–500 orders a day [83]. Huxiu reported the Meituan pharmacy cooperation expanding from 7 Beijing stores to 100 in Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen [63].

## 7. Hong Kong

**2024: HKIC investment.** On 19 July 2024 HKIC announced an investment in Galbot and an "ecosystem expansion" partnership at a forum witnessed by Paul Chan [8]. It was HKIC's third AI investment after SmartMore (思謀科技) and BioMap (百圖生科) [40]. Galbot committed to an international embodied-AI talent exchange centre with Hong Kong universities, a "HK-Galbot Embodied AI Lab", training over 100 young people a year and 500 within five years, and to trial robots in retail and tourism [84, 85]. Clara Chan said Galbot would consider Hong Kong as its preferred listing venue [86]. Wang He said then that he hoped robots would be working in Hong Kong shops within one to two years [86]. HKIC also took part in the November 2024 round [48].

**Local presence.** Galbot's chief strategy officer said it has had a Hong Kong office since 2024 [54]. Wen Wei Po reported it entered Hong Kong Science Park (香港科學園) in 2025 and has R&D centres in Beijing, Shenzhen, Suzhou and Hong Kong [16].

**2026: Galbot Store.** A launch ceremony co-organised by HKIC was held at Kai Tak Arts Centre (啟德體藝館), Kai Tak Sports Park, on 31 August 2026; Paul Chan made the first purchase [9, 56]. The first three stores opened on 1 September 2026 [9]:
- Hung Hom waterfront (紅磡新海濱), supported by Bauhinia Culture Group [57];
- Wan Chai waterfront (灣仔海濱, water-sports area), at the Cross-Harbour Tunnel end [70, 87];
- Kai Tak Arts Centre ground floor, Kai Tak Sports Park [16].

A fourth store opened on the Avenue of Stars (星光大道), Tsim Sha Tsui, at kiosk HS18 near the McDull statue, open 10:00–22:00 [88, 55]. SCMP reported plans for 10 more stores in the city [54]; Wang He told Commercial Radio there could be "10 to several dozen" if sales grow [17].

Details from opening coverage:
- Goods: drinks, snacks, Hong Kong-themed souvenirs (pineapple-bun and egg-tart merchandise), priced HK$14–139; payment by card, WeChat Pay, Alipay or UnionPay [89]. At Wan Chai a 「李小艾」 lime drink was HK$14 and other drinks HK$18 [87].
- The robot speaks Cantonese, Mandarin and English, according to Galbot [26].
- Ta Kung Pao reported that Paul Chan had to repeat a Cantonese question three times before the robot answered correctly, and that the robot sometimes failed to pick items at the back of shelves and wrongly treated them as delivered [70].
- HK01 timed a water purchase at 2 minutes 16 seconds; the robot did not understand a spoken request for Evian, and staff said orders must go through the tablet screen; card payment was not working on day one [87].
- SCMP headlined heavy rain and internet outages on opening day [89].
- Wang He said the field is still young and robots still struggle with tasks they meet for the first time; he expects base-model capability around "GPT-3.5 to GPT-4 level" by about 2028 [70].
- An HKEJ tech column headline said the store robot "will learn Cantonese well" (零售店機械人將學好廣東話) [90].

**HKPC hub.** On 15 October 2026 the Hong Kong Productivity Council's FLAIR centre (香港工業人工智能與機械人研發中心) opened the 具身智能國際應用訓練場 (Embodied AI International Applications Hub) at the HKPC Building, Kowloon Tong, with seven technology partners including Unitree and Galbot and eight "scenario partners" [58, 55]. Galbot said it will run a training camp on secondary development with HKPC [55].

## 9. Controversies and open issues

**Listing plans.** On 9 December 2025 Galbot denied reports that it would list in Hong Kong and file in January 2026, saying the share reform was needed because it had over 50 shareholders and wanted to bring in new private investors [64, 12, 96]. On 12 December 2025 Bloomberg reported that it had picked CITIC Securities, Huatai Securities and UBS for a Hong Kong IPO and might file as soon as the next quarter [13]; 智通財經 gave a target valuation of USD 3–4 billion [97]. Hong Kong Economic Journal headlined the report 「銀河通用傳港掛牌 尋求估值312億」 [98]. On 5 August 2026 SCMP cited a source saying Galbot had filed confidentially in Hong Kong [11]. A Sina list dated 26 September 2026 rated an August confidential filing as "medium" credibility and unconfirmed [10]. As of this brief, neither Galbot nor HKEX has confirmed a filing. At the World Robot Conference in August 2026 Wang He did not answer questions about listing progress [59]; in Hong Kong in September he said the market "can expect good news" [17].

**Mech-Mind criticism (September 2026).** Mech-Mind (梅卡曼德, 09615.HK) listed in Hong Kong on 1 September 2026 at HK$101.70 and fell below its offer price on day one [99]. On 9–10 September its founder Shao Tianlan wrote in WeChat Moments that some "攢局型" ("deal-assembling") embodied-AI companies, "in Beijing and Shanghai, famous, highly valued, on the Spring Festival Gala", used data-collection centres and related-party deals with local governments, investors and suppliers to create "false, unsustainable revenue", and in comments named Galbot [59, 6]. Galbot replied on 10 September with an article titled 《具身智能是一場長跑，我們只與時間賽跑》, saying it would "not take part in pointless slanging matches" (不參與無謂的口水戰) [100]. 21st Century Business Herald reported that Galbot is itself a Mech-Mind customer [59].

**Anonymous allegation material.** On 11 September 2026 a six-page document titled 《銀河通用港股上市的變身術與遮眼法》 circulated [101]. It alleged that Yin Fangming owes nearly RMB 100 million under a share-buyback arbitration over ROOBO and was placed under a consumption restriction; that Galbot's actual controller changed three times (his mother 郭小亮, then 姚騰洲 in January 2026, then Yin as chairman in July 2026); and that the confidential-filing route under Chapter 18C was being used to delay disclosure [43, 101]. On 12 September Galbot said the content was "malicious rumours", untrue, and reported to the police [6]. Caizhongshe noted that Galbot did not answer the points one by one [102].

**Management change.** The registry change filed 21 July 2026 removed ten directors and supervisors (including chairman 郭小亮 and director 陳維廣, a managing partner of Lanchi Ventures [8]) and added nine (including 尹方鳴, 孫登科, 王鶴 and 張直政); 姚騰洲 stayed as manager and legal representative [46].
