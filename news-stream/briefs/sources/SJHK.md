# Background brief: Hong Kong's Secretary for Justice Paul Lam Ting-kwok (林定國), his deputy and the Department of Justice (律政司)

Status as of 5 November 2026. Every factual statement carries a numbered citation; the source list is at the end.

---

## 1. What the subject is, in one paragraph

Hong Kong is a Special Administrative Region (SAR, 特別行政區) of the People's Republic of China, governed under the Basic Law (《基本法》) and the "One Country, Two Systems" (一國兩制) arrangement, with its own common-law legal system. The Secretary for Justice (律政司司長, abbreviated "SJ") is one of the three top-ranking "Secretaries of Department" (司長) in the Hong Kong government, after the Chief Secretary for Administration and the Financial Secretary; the post is nominated by the Chief Executive (行政長官) and appointed by China's State Council (國務院) [10]. The SJ heads the Department of Justice (律政司, "DoJ"), the government's law office: it brings criminal prosecutions, drafts government bills and advises the government on law [10]. Article 63 of the Basic Law states that the DoJ "shall control criminal prosecutions, free from any interference" [11]. Since 1 July 2022 the SJ has been Paul Lam Ting-kwok (林定國), a barrister and Senior Counsel, and his deputy has been Cheung Kwok-kwan (張國鈞, English name Horace Cheung) [1][3][5]. Under the 2020 Hong Kong National Security Law (香港國安法), the SJ also sits on Hong Kong's Committee for Safeguarding National Security, and the DoJ prosecutes national security cases through a specialised division [13][14].

### 2.4 Things easily confused with it

- **律政司 as a person vs. as a department.** Before 1997 the colonial Attorney General was himself called 律政司, and his department was 律政署 (Legal Department) or 律政司署 (Attorney General's Chambers). Since 1997 the post is 律政司司長 and 律政司 means the department [10]. Older texts saying "律政司" may mean the person.
- **The US Department of Justice (DOJ)**, which shares the English name.
- **The Judiciary (司法機構)**, which is separate: DoJ lawyers prosecute; judges decide. National security cases are heard by judges designated for them, and an SJ certificate can direct a trial without a jury before three judges [12].
- **The Security Bureau (保安局)** under Secretary for Security Chris Tang (鄧炳強) and the **Police Force's department for safeguarding national security** investigate and arrest; DoJ decides on and conducts prosecutions [62][15].
- **The other two "Secretaries"**: Chief Secretary Eric Chan Kwok-ki (陳國基) and Financial Secretary Paul Chan (陳茂波) [3]. "Paul Lam" (林定國) is also not former Chief Executive Carrie Lam (林鄭月娥) [37].
- **Teresa Cheng Yeuk-wah (鄭若驊)**, Lam's predecessor as SJ (2018–2022), is now Secretary-General of the International Organization for Mediation (IOMed) in Hong Kong [10][42].
- **IOMed (國際調解院)** is the International Organization for Mediation, not the International Organization for Migration (IOM) [41].
- **Mainland bodies**: China's Ministry of Justice (司法部) and the procuratorates (檢察院) are mainland institutions; Hong Kong's DoJ is not part of them. DoJ does work with them (for example, joint training with the Ministry of Justice) [74].

## 3. Paul Lam: biography

- **Born** 26 March 1968 in Hong Kong [2].
- **Education**: Wah Yan College, Hong Kong; LLB (1990) and PCLL (1991) from the University of Hong Kong; LLM in international law from the University of Nottingham (1992) [2].
- **Private practice**: called to the Hong Kong Bar in 1992; general civil and commercial practice at Parkside Chambers, of which he became head in 2018; appointed Senior Counsel in 2013 [1][2].
- **Bar Association (香港大律師公會)**: Vice-Chairman 2014–2017 and Chairman 2017–2018 (government biography) [1].
- **Part-time judge**: sat as a Deputy Judge of the High Court for short periods each year from 2015 [1].
- **Mainland links**: arbitrator on the panels of the Shenzhen Court of International Arbitration and CIETAC; appointed professor by the China University of Political Science and Law; passed the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area Legal Professional Examination [1].
- **Public service before government**: Chairman of the Consumer Council, Chairman of the Administrative Appeals Board, Chairman of the ICAC Operations Review Committee, member of the Independent Police Complaints Council [1].
- **Appointment**: on 19 June 2022 the Central People's Government, on the nomination of Chief Executive-elect John Lee (李家超), appointed him Secretary for Justice of the sixth-term government, replacing Teresa Cheng, from 1 July 2022 [3][4]. He is the fifth SJ since 1997, after Elsie Leung (梁愛詩, 1997–2005), Wong Yan-lung (黃仁龍, 2005–2012), Rimsky Yuen (袁國強, 2012–2018) and Teresa Cheng (2018–2022) [10].
- **Other roles of the office**: the SJ is an Official Member of the Executive Council (行政會議) [7], chairs the Law Reform Commission (法律改革委員會), and is an ex officio member of the Judicial Officers Recommendation Commission (司法人員推薦委員會) [10]. The SJ is third in line to act as Chief Executive; Lam acted as Chief Executive on 23 April 2025 [10].
- **Honours**: Silver Bauhinia Star (2021) [2]; Gold Bauhinia Star on the 1 July 2025 Honours List [38].

## 4. Cheung Kwok-kwan (Horace Cheung): the deputy

- Deputy Secretary for Justice since 1 July 2022, appointed at the same time as Lam; the DSJ post was created with that government [3][6].
- Before government: solicitor in private practice, China-appointed Attesting Officer and civil celebrant of marriages; non-official member of the Executive Council; member of the Legislative Council and of a District Council [5]. He was a vice-chairman of the Democratic Alliance for the Betterment and Progress of Hong Kong (DAB, 民建聯) from 2011 to 2022 and sat in the Legislative Council from 2016 to 2022 [6].
- He is an Official Member of the Executive Council [7].
- In March 2026 he spoke for Hong Kong, as part of the Chinese delegation, at the 61st session of the UN Human Rights Council in Geneva, saying that implementing the National Security Law had safeguarded national security and ensured stability [53].
- He vice-chairs DoJ's preparatory group for aligning with China's 15th Five-Year Plan (set up 20 February 2026) [50] and spoke at several events of Hong Kong Legal Week 2026 [65].
- In May 2026 he confirmed to the press that Lam had sent staff an internal memo about allegations against the new DPP, and warned media that asking about "unsubstantiated allegations" would fuel them [20].

According to DoJ's organisation page (revised 8 September 2026), DoJ lawyers work in six legal divisions, each headed by a "Law Officer", supported by an Administration and Development Division [8]:

| Post | Holder named on the DoJ chart (Sept 2026) |
|---|---|

| Deputy Secretary for Justice | Dr Cheung Kwok-kwan, SBS, JP [8] |
| Consultant (Special Duty), Special Duty Team | Christina Cheung, SBS, JP [8] |
| Law Officer (Civil Law), acting | Clifford Tavares [8] |
| Law Officer (International Law) | Dr James Ding, JP [8] |
| Law Draftsman | Michael Lam, JP [8] |
| Solicitor General, acting | Llewellyn Mui, JP [8] |
| Law Officer (National Security), National Security Prosecutions Division | No name shown on the chart [8] |
| Director of Public Prosecutions | Anthony Chau (周天行) [8][18] |
| Director of Administration and Development | Amy Yuen, JP [8] |

In a February 2026 DoJ photo caption Christina Cheung was still described as Law Officer (Civil Law) [50]. The department's address is Justice Place (律政中心), 18 Lower Albert Road, Central [8].

**Prosecutions.** The DPP, ranked at Law Officer (DL6) level, directs public prosecutions and advises on criminal law [18]. DoJ's own Prosecution Code says a prosecutor must not be influenced by political, media or community interests [11a]. DoJ describes Basic Law Article 63 as entrenching prosecutorial independence, with the SJ acting in a "quasi-judicial capacity" when deciding whether to prosecute [11].

**National security prosecutions.** Article 18 of the National Security Law requires DoJ to set up a specialised prosecution division for national security offences; its prosecutors are appointed by the SJ with the consent of the Committee for Safeguarding National Security, and its head is appointed by the Chief Executive after seeking in writing the opinion of the central government's Office for Safeguarding National Security [13]. DoJ said on 30 June–1 July 2020 that it had already set up such a unit [16]. Article 46 lets the SJ issue a certificate directing that a national security trial in the Court of First Instance be heard without a jury by three judges, on grounds including protection of state secrets, foreign factors and jurors' safety [12]. Article 13 makes the SJ a member of the Committee for Safeguarding National Security (國家安全委員會), chaired by the Chief Executive [14][15].

**Policy and legislation.** DoJ drafts all government bills [10]. The LEAD Office is DoJ's central policy unit for rule-of-law education, promotion of Hong Kong's legal and dispute-resolution services, and legal integration with the Greater Bay Area [17]. DoJ runs the annual Hong Kong Legal Week [65].

**International legal cooperation.** DoJ has secondment arrangements with the Hague Conference on Private International Law, UNIDROIT, UNCITRAL, IOMed and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, and runs "Belt and Road" visits for young lawyers with the Office of the Commissioner of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Hong Kong [68].

## 6. Timeline

### Before Lam

- **1997**: the colonial Attorney General post (律政司) becomes Secretary for Justice (律政司司長) [10].
- **2002**: the post becomes a political appointment rather than a civil-service post [10].
- **30 June 2020**: the National Security Law takes effect. DoJ under Teresa Cheng says it has set up a specialised national security prosecution unit [16]. The Committee for Safeguarding National Security is set up on 3 July 2020 [15].

### 2022

- **19 June**: the State Council appoints Lam SJ and Cheung DSJ [3][4].
- **1 July**: both take office [1][5].
- **July**: the US Congressional-Executive Commission on China recommends sanctions on Lam; Lam says he is not concerned and compares the US to the cartoon bully Gian [2].
- **13 August**: Lam signs a certificate ordering that the "Hong Kong 47" subversion case be tried without a jury, citing "involvement of foreign factors" and jurors' safety [21].
- **22 August**: AFP reports Lam has also ordered a no-jury trial for Jimmy Lai (黎智英) [22].
- **October–November**: DoJ tries in the courts to stop Lai hiring British barrister Timothy Owen KC and loses at each level; on 28 November the Court of Final Appeal refuses DoJ leave to appeal [2][24].
- **1 December**: Lam issues a certificate for a no-jury trial of three leaders of the Hong Kong Alliance (支聯會) [23].
- **30 December**: at John Lee's request, China's National People's Congress Standing Committee (NPCSC, 全國人大常委會) interprets the National Security Law to say the Chief Executive's certificate is needed for overseas lawyers in national security cases [24][25].

### 2023

- **10 May**: the Legislative Council unanimously passes the Legal Practitioners (Amendment) Bill 2023, giving the Chief Executive the say over overseas lawyers in national security cases [26].
- **June–July**: DoJ applies for an injunction against the protest song "Glory to Hong Kong" (《願榮光歸香港》); the Court of First Instance refuses it in July [2].
- **1 November**: a bipartisan US bill proposes sanctions on 49 Hong Kong officials, judges and prosecutors, including Lam [34a]; on 4 November Lam calls it a "bully" move, repeating the Gian/Nobita comparison [35].

### 2024

- **19 March**: the Legislative Council unanimously passes the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (Article 23 legislation); it takes effect on 23 March [29].
- **8 May**: the Court of Appeal allows the SJ's appeal and grants the "Glory to Hong Kong" injunction [27][28].
- **30 May**: 14 of the 16 "Hong Kong 47" defendants who stood trial are convicted and two acquitted [31].
- **19 November**: 45 defendants are sentenced; Benny Tai (戴耀廷) receives 10 years and others four years two months to seven years nine months [30][31].

### 2025

- **31 March**: the US State Department sanctions six officials, including Lam, under Executive Order 13936, saying he was "responsible for or involved in developing, adopting, or implementing" the National Security Law; the others were Sonny Au (區志光, Secretary-General of the Committee for Safeguarding National Security), Dong Jingwei (董經緯, head of the central Office for Safeguarding National Security), Police Commissioner Raymond Siu (蕭澤頤) and two national security police assistant commissioners [32].
- **6 April**: Lam says no sanctioned official has left their post because of sanctions [34].
- **23 April**: Lam acts as Chief Executive [10].
- **30 May**: the Convention establishing IOMed, headquartered in Hong Kong, is signed by 33 founding states [41].
- **June**: the "foreign collusion" charge against Joshua Wong (黃之鋒) is brought [69].
- **30 June**: on the National Security Law's fifth anniversary Lam says "soft resistance" is a political, not legal, term, and describes it as making false or misleading statements with emotional bias to create misunderstanding of the authorities [39][40].
- **1 July**: Lam receives the Gold Bauhinia Star [38].
- **20 October**: IOMed is inaugurated in Hong Kong; Teresa Cheng is its first Secretary-General [42].
- **26 November**: fire at Wang Fuk Court (宏福苑), Tai Po; 168 people die [63].
- **1–5 December**: Hong Kong Legal Week 2025 [73].
- **15 December**: the Court of First Instance convicts Jimmy Lai of two counts of conspiracy to collude with foreign forces and one of conspiracy to publish seditious publications [43].

### 2026

- **9 February**: Lai is sentenced to 20 years, the longest sentence under the National Security Law so far; the bench was Esther Toh, Susana D'Almada Remedios and Alex Lee; eight co-defendants receive 6 to 10 years [44][45]. The US State Department calls the sentence "unjust" and asks for humanitarian parole [46].
- **10 February**: China's State Council Information Office issues a white paper, "Hong Kong: Safeguarding China's National Security Under the Framework of One Country, Two Systems" [49].
- **20 February**: DoJ sets up a high-level preparatory group, chaired by Lam and vice-chaired by Cheung, to align with China's 15th Five-Year Plan [50].
- **23 February**: the Court of Appeal dismisses the appeals of 12 "Hong Kong 47" defendants against conviction or sentence, and dismisses DoJ's appeal against the acquittal of barrister Lawrence Lau (劉偉聰) [51][52].
- **26 February**: the Court of Appeal quashes Lai's separate 2022 fraud conviction (over the Apple Daily office lease) and its 69-month sentence [48].
- **5–6 March**: Lai's lawyers say he will not appeal his national security conviction or sentence [48].
- **18 March**: Cheung addresses the UN Human Rights Council [53].
- **23 March**: amendments to the Implementation Rules for Article 43 of the National Security Law are gazetted; the government says they strengthen law-enforcement powers, with the courts as gatekeeper [54]. They include a power to require a suspect to provide device passwords [55].
- **15 April**: at a National Security Legal Forum on National Security Education Day, Lam says Hong Kong seeks "open security" (開放型安全), not "generalised" security [57][20].
- **20–21 May**: Deputy DPP Anthony Chau (周天行), prosecutor in the 47 and Jimmy Lai cases, succeeds Maggie Yang (楊美琪) as DPP after her 32 years at DoJ [18][19].
- **23 May**: after online posts by overseas activist Frances Hui alleged that Chau had an affair with a subordinate and misused public funds, DoJ says the allegations are "completely without factual basis", that an earlier anonymous complaint was investigated, and that the matter is referred to the police [19][20].
- **26–27 May**: Lam's internal memo asks staff not to repost the allegations and to report information about their source; Cheung confirms the memo [20].
- **9 June**: the Safeguarding National Security (Procedural Matters) Regulation takes effect: a Chief Executive certificate that an act involves national security makes the case one "concerning offence endangering national security" [58].
- **14–17 July**: the US national emergency on Hong Kong under Executive Order 13936 is allowed to lapse. On 17 July OFAC delists the people sanctioned under it, and nine, including Lam, Dong Jingwei and former police commissioners Raymond Siu and Stephen Lo (盧偉聰), are not moved to the remaining Hong Kong Autonomy Act list; 39 others, including John Lee, stay sanctioned [36][37].
- **21 August**: Lee Cheuk-yan (李卓人) and Chow Hang-tung (鄒幸彤) of the Hong Kong Alliance are convicted of inciting subversion; Albert Ho (何俊仁) had pleaded guilty [59][60].
- **2 September**: Joshua Wong pleads guilty to conspiracy to collude with foreign forces [69].
- **11 September**: Lee is sentenced to 7 years, Chow to 7 years 3 months, Ho to 5 years 2 months [59].
- **16 September**: John Lee delivers his fifth Policy Address together with Hong Kong's first Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) [61].
- **16 October**: Lam attends the Hainan Free Trade Port Legal Week [72].
- **19 October**: Lam leaves for Brunei for the 16th China-ASEAN Prosecutors-General Conference [71].
- **29 October**: the independent committee's report on the Wang Fuk Court fire is published; officials bow in apology; Secretary for Housing Winnie Ho (何永賢) resigns; parts of the report are redacted because of pending criminal trials [63]. At the same press conference Lam announces plans to amend three laws to make bid-rigging in major building-maintenance works a criminal offence (up to 10 years' jail and a HK$10 million fine, three times the gain, or 50% of yearly local turnover) [62].
- **1 November**: on radio Lam calls the reforms a "revolutionary overhaul" of the building-maintenance sector [64].
- **2–6 November**: Hong Kong Legal Week 2026, theme "Collaboration & Innovation: Legal Synergy for a Shared Future"; UNIDROIT opens its first regional office, an Asia-Pacific Liaison Office, which Lam says will sit in the "Hong Kong Legal Hub" in Central [65][66][67].
- **5 November**: Joshua Wong is sentenced to 10 years by Judge William Tam; the court treats the offence as of a "grave nature" [69][70]. On the same day Lam's closing remarks at Legal Week mention planned amendments to the Arbitration Ordinance and mediation law, and a Hong Kong International Legal Service Building beside IOMed headquarters [68].

## 7. The main areas of work and where they stand now

### 7.1 National security prosecutions

DoJ prosecutes all national security cases, under both the 2020 National Security Law and the 2024 Safeguarding National Security Ordinance [13][29]. Under Lam, SJ certificates under Article 46 have sent the largest cases (the 47, Jimmy Lai, the Hong Kong Alliance) to trial without a jury [21][22][23]. Outcomes in 2026:

- **Jimmy Lai and Apple Daily**: convicted 15 December 2025, sentenced to 20 years on 9 February 2026, no appeal [43][44][48]. Co-defendant Fung Wai-kong, former editor of Apple Daily's English section, lodged an appeal [48].
- **"Hong Kong 47"**: appeals of 12 defendants dismissed on 23 February 2026; DoJ's appeal against Lawrence Lau's acquittal also dismissed [51][52].
- **Hong Kong Alliance (支聯會案)**: convictions 21 August, sentences 11 September 2026; Lee's wife said he would appeal [59].
- **Joshua Wong**: 10 years on 5 November 2026, his second National Security Law conviction; before the new charge he had been due for release in January 2027 [69][70]. HRW says he faces prison until late 2036 [69].

Supporting legal changes made in 2026: the Article 43 Implementation Rules amendments (March) and the SNSO Procedural Matters Regulation (June) [54][58]. The government says these do not affect law-abiding people; HRW and other critics describe a pattern of stacking charges to keep prominent activists in prison [54][58][69].

### 7.2 The Director of Public Prosecutions controversy

Anthony Chau became DPP on 21 May 2026 [18]. He was called to the Bar on 28 February 2026, after joining DoJ in 2007 as a solicitor [18][19]. The allegations against him, the DoJ's rejection of them, the police referral and Lam's staff memo are set out in the timeline [19][20]. As of 28 May 2026 neither Lam nor Cheung had said whether DoJ would look into the allegations themselves [20]. Later developments in the police referral could not be confirmed for this brief.

### 7.3 Sanctions

Lam was sanctioned by the US from 31 March 2025 to 17 July 2026 [32][36][37]. In Hong Kong the government's reply to the delisting described the US move as an important step in carrying out the China–US trade consensus [37].

### 7.4 Legal policy and legislation

- Overseas lawyers in national security cases (2022–2023): the NPCSC interpretation and the Legal Practitioners (Amendment) Ordinance [24][26].
- "Glory to Hong Kong" injunction (2023–2024) [27][28].
- Building-maintenance reforms after the Wang Fuk Court fire: amendments to the Competition Ordinance, the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance and the ICAC Ordinance, with the Interception of Communications and Surveillance Ordinance also to apply; the Secretary for Commerce expected the bills to pass around April 2027 [62][64]. DoJ is also helping amend the Buildings Ordinance, Fire Services Ordinance and other building laws [62].
- Arbitration and mediation: consultation on amending the Arbitration Ordinance ended in late October 2026 and mediation-law changes are planned under the Five-Year Plan [68].

### 7.5 International legal hub and cooperation

The government's stated aim is to develop Hong Kong as an "international legal and dispute resolution services centre" and, in Lam's words, "the only common law jurisdiction within China" [68]. Concrete items:

- IOMed, headquartered in Hong Kong (Convention signed 30 May 2025; opened 20 October 2025) [41][42].
- UNIDROIT Asia-Pacific Liaison Office, opened 2 November 2026 [65][67].
- The Judiciary's plan for a Hong Kong International Commercial Court (HKICC), described at Legal Week 2026 by Chief Judge of the High Court Jeremy Poon [68].
- A planned Hong Kong International Legal Service Building next to the IOMed headquarters [68].
- Training through the Hong Kong International Legal Talents Training Academy, running courses since at least January 2025 [74].
- Regular trips abroad and to the mainland: for example Hainan (October 2026) and Brunei (October 2026) [71][72].

### 7.6 Rule of law messaging and national development

Lam's speeches link DoJ's work to China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) and Hong Kong's first Five-Year Plan [50][61][68]. On national security he has said Hong Kong seeks "open" and "relative" security balanced against development [57]. DoJ also runs national-security and Constitution/Basic Law education, including the National Security Legal Forum each April [20][57].

## 9. Ongoing debates and controversies

Stated as positions held by named parties, not as findings:

1. **Prosecutorial independence vs. national security priorities.** DoJ and the government cite Basic Law Article 63 and state that prosecutions are evidence-based [11][70]. Critics such as HRW say charges are stacked against prominent activists and call the charges "baseless" [69]. The US State Department called the Lai sentence "unjust" [46].
2. **Trials without juries.** Lam's Article 46 certificates cited foreign factors and juror safety [21][23]. AFP noted that trial by jury had been used in Hong Kong for 177 years [22].
3. **Overseas lawyers.** DoJ lost four times in court before the NPCSC interpretation and the 2023 amendment [24][26].
4. **"Soft resistance."** Lam's 2025 definition (false or misleading statements, emotional bias, intent to create misunderstanding) drew commentary that the concept remains unclear [39][40].
5. **Reporting restrictions.** HRW notes that a 2024 law lets courts, on national security grounds, refuse a defendant's request to lift reporting restrictions on committal proceedings, so the details of Wong's charge were not public until his plea [69].
6. **The DPP allegations.** DoJ calls them smears and referred them to police; HKFP reported that the leadership did not say whether the allegations themselves would be investigated, and that Lam asked staff to report the source [19][20].
7. **Accountability for the Wang Fuk Court fire.** Only one political appointee (Winnie Ho) left office; residents and families quoted by BBC Chinese said the apology came late and accountability was not enough [63].
8. **US sanctions.** The 2026 delisting followed China–US trade talks, while 39 people remain sanctioned under the Hong Kong Autonomy Act [36][37].

## 10. How to recognise a post about this topic

Typical headline patterns (examples taken from the sources):

- 「律政司司長林定國表示……」 / "Secretary for Justice Paul Lam said…" — any statement by Lam [62][68].
- 「律政司發聲明」 / "DoJ said in a statement" — usually about a prosecution, a court ruling, or rebutting criticism [19].
- 「控方」「主控官周天行」 — prosecution side in a trial report [19].
- 「國安法」「23條」「勾結外國勢力」「煽惑顛覆」「串謀發布煽動刊物」 — national security charges (collusion with foreign forces, incitement to subversion, seditious publications) [43][59].
- 「圍標刑事化」 — the post-fire bid-rigging bill [62].
- 「香港法律周」 / "Hong Kong Legal Week"; 「國際調解院」 / "IOMed"; 「國際統一私法協會」 / "UNIDROIT"; 「十五五」 / "15th Five-Year Plan"; 「第一個五年規劃」 / "First Five-Year Plan" [42][65][66][68].
- 「制裁」 with 林定國 — US sanctions (2025) and their lifting (July 2026) [32][37].
- 「開放型安全」「軟對抗」 — Lam's national-security vocabulary [39][57].
