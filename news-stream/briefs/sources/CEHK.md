# Background brief: Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu (李家超) and the HKSAR government he leads

*Status as of 22 October 2026. Every factual claim has a numbered source; the source list is at the end. Where something is speculation or commentary, it is labelled as such.*

---

## 1. What the subject is

Hong Kong is a Special Administrative Region (SAR) of the People's Republic of China (香港特別行政區, HKSAR). Its head is the **Chief Executive (行政長官)**. The Chief Executive is chosen by a 1,500-member Election Committee (選舉委員會) and then appointed by the Central People's Government in Beijing, for a five-year term [75].

**John Lee Ka-chiu (李家超)** is the sixth-term Chief Executive. He was elected on 8 May 2022, appointed by the Central People's Government on 20 May 2022, and took office on 1 July 2022 [1]. Chinese President Xi Jinping swore him in [6]. His current term runs from 1 July 2022 to 30 June 2027 [70]. He also chairs the HKSAR's Committee for Safeguarding National Security (國家安全委員會, often shortened to 國安委) [7].

Posts about "the Chief Executive", "the government" or "the SAR government" usually involve one or more of these bodies:

- **The Chief Executive's Office (行政長官辦公室, 特首辦, CEO)**: the CE's own office. Its director is **Carol Yip Man-kuen (葉文娟)**. Its Permanent Secretary is Hinny Lam Shuk-yee [14]. The office's sections include a Press Section, a Communications Team and the Executive Council Secretariat [14].
- **The Executive Council (行政會議, 行會, ExCo)**: the body that advises the CE on policy. The CE presides. It has 21 official members (the principal officials) and 16 non-official members [10].
- **The principal officials (主要官員, 問責官員, "secretaries"/局長)**: the three Secretaries of Departments (司長), three deputies (副司長) and 15 bureau heads (局長). All are nominated by the CE and appointed by the Central People's Government [17][18].
- **Advisory and policy bodies attached to the CE**: the Chief Executive's Policy Unit (特首政策組), headed by **Dr Stephen Wong Yuen-shan (黃元山)** [15], and the Chief Executive's Council of Advisers (特首顧問團), which the CE chairs [16].

### 2.2 John Lee himself

- **English:** John Lee Ka-chiu; John Lee; "Mr John Lee" (the government's usual style); "John KC Lee" (his signature on Policy Address documents) [82]. US sanctions lists also record "LEE, Ka Chiu" and "LI, Jiachao" (the Mandarin pinyin form) [3].
- **Chinese:** 李家超. Honorifics after his name: 大紫荊勳賢 (GBM), SBS, PDSM, PMSM [11].
- **Nicknames recorded in Chinese Wikipedia:** 十一哥, 比卡超, 沙塵超, 頁問, 超哥 and "Lady Killer" [7].
  - **比卡超** is the Hong Kong Cantonese name of the Pokémon Pikachu. "Lee Ka-chiu" sounds like it, and it is described as his best-known nickname [9].
  - **頁問** is a play on 葉問 (Ip Man, the Wing Chun grandmaster). His former boss, ex-Secretary for Security Lai Tung-kwok (黎棟國), revealed it on radio in 2022. Lee has practised qigong for 20 years [8].
  - **超哥** ("Brother Chiu") is used on the LIHKG forum (連登) [78].
- **A quote forum users still repeat:** at a 2019 press conference, asked about a protester shot in the eye, Lee said 「佢唔喺現場出現就冇事，去得嗰度就可能有呢啲風險」 ("If he hadn't been at the scene nothing would have happened; going there carries such risks"). LIHKG users have reused it as a meme [9].

### 2.3 Other recurring terms

- **施政報告** = Policy Address, the CE's annual programme speech to the Legislative Council (立法會, LegCo) [52].
- **《香港第一個五年規劃》 / 「一五」規劃** = Hong Kong's First Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) [49]. **「十五五」** = the national 15th Five-Year Plan [49].
- **「四中心、一高地」** = "four centres and one hub". The four centres are international finance, trade, shipping/aviation, and innovation and technology; the hub is for high-calibre talent [62][49].
- **北部都會區 / 北都** = the Northern Metropolis, near the Shenzhen border [53].
- **「愛國者治港」** = "patriots administering Hong Kong", the principle behind Beijing's 2021 overhaul of the electoral system [42][74].
- **行政主導** = "executive-led" government [49][62].
- **宏福苑大火 / 大埔五級火 / 大埔火災** = the Wang Fuk Court fire in Tai Po, 26 November 2025 (§6) [31].
- **選委會** = Election Committee; **資審會** = Candidate Eligibility Review Committee (候選人資格審查委員會) [75].
- **收風** = Beijing "gathering soundings" on officials' performance (§7) [68].
- **「10年魔咒」** ("ten-year curse") = the fact that no Chief Executive since 1997 has served two full five-year terms [68][69].

## 3. John Lee: biography

- **Birth and family.** Born 7 December 1957 [3][7]. His ancestral home is Panyu (番禺), Guangzhou [7]. He grew up in So Uk Estate (蘇屋邨), a public housing estate in Sham Shui Po [79]. His wife is Janet Lam Lai-sim (林麗嬋), married 1980, and they have two sons [7]. Chinese Wikipedia lists his religion as Roman Catholic [7].
- **Education.** He attended 五邑工商總會學校 primary school and Wah Yan College, Kowloon (九龍華仁書院) [7]. He holds a master's degree in public policy and management from Charles Sturt University, Australia [1].
- **Police career, 1977–2012.** He joined the Royal Hong Kong Police as a probationary inspector in 1977 [1]. Posts he held included Assistant Commissioner (Crime), Director of Crime and Security, and Deputy Commissioner (Management), the last from 2010 [1][7]. Mainland media credit him with work on the 1998 case against the gangster Cheung Tze-keung (張子強) while he led the Organised Crime and Triad Bureau (O記) [92].
- **Political career.**
  - Under Secretary for Security, 2012 [1].
  - Secretary for Security, 2017 to 2021, the first former police officer in that post [1][92]. He held it during the 2019 anti-extradition-bill protests (反修例 / 反送中運動) [9].
  - Chief Secretary for Administration, 25 June 2021 to 7 April 2022, and first chairman of the Candidate Eligibility Review Committee [7].
- **US sanctions.** On 7 August 2020, while he was Secretary for Security, the US Treasury sanctioned him under Executive Order 13936. Ten others were sanctioned at the same time, including Carrie Lam, Chris Tang, Erick Tsang, Xia Baolong and Eric Chan [2]. The EO 13936 national emergency expired on 14 July 2026. On 17 July 2026 OFAC removed people sanctioned only under that order from its SDN list. Lee is still sanctioned under the Hong Kong Autonomy Act (HKAA): he was moved to OFAC's Non-SDN Menu-Based Sanctions List, and his property remains blocked. Chris Tang and Eric Chan were moved the same way [3].
- **2022 election.** He was the sole candidate. He received 1,416 "support" votes and 8 "not support" votes, out of 1,428 ballots cast by the 1,461-member Election Committee [4][5]. Members of that committee had been vetted by the eligibility review committee he chaired [4].

## 5. Lee's term to date: main events

### 2022–2024

- **Article 23 law.** On 19 March 2024 LegCo passed the **Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (《維護國家安全條例》)**, the local law required by Basic Law Article 23. Lee called the day "a historic moment" [23]. The vote was unanimous and the bill passed within two weeks of being tabled [24]. The ordinance took effect on 23 March 2024 [23].
- **Third Policy Address.** Delivered 16 October 2024 under the title "Reform for Enhancing Development and Building Our Future Together" [87].

### 2025

- **US sanctions on six officials (31 March 2025).** The US sanctioned six Chinese and Hong Kong officials, including Secretary for Justice Paul Lam, Dong Jingwei, Sonny Au and former Police Commissioner Raymond Siu (蕭澤頤) [83].
- **Liaison Office change (30 May 2025).** Zhou Ji replaced Zheng Yanxiong as Liaison Office director [84].
- **Same-sex partnerships bill (10 September 2025).** LegCo voted down the government's Registration of Same-sex Partnerships Bill by 71 to 14. The government had introduced it to comply with a court ruling [25].
- **Fourth Policy Address (17 September 2025).** Lee said his "ultimate objective in governance" was to improve people's livelihoods [26]. He announced:
  - a committee he would chair to speed up the Northern Metropolis [26][27];
  - a system to hold senior civil servants accountable, with possible pay cuts, compulsory retirement or dismissal [27].
- **Wang Fuk Court fire (26 November 2025).** See §6.
- **LegCo election (7 December 2025).** Held as scheduled after the fire [41].
- **Jimmy Lai convicted (15 December 2025).** Lai Chee-ying (黎智英) was convicted of national security and sedition offences [47].

### 2026

- **Erick Tsang resigns (27 January).** See §4.1 [18][19].
- **Jimmy Lai sentenced (9 February).** He received 20 years. Lee said the sentence showed "the rule of law is upheld and justice is done" [45][46].
- **2026-27 Budget (25 February).** Financial Secretary Paul Chan said the Operating Account had returned to surplus in 2025-26, ahead of schedule. He put fiscal reserves at HK$657.2 billion at end-March 2026 [48].
- **Janice Tse appointed (30 March).** See §4.1 [21].
- **Five-Year Plan.** Chan Kwok-ki, Paul Chan and Janice Tse travelled to Beijing in April 2026 for talks with the HKMAO on the plan [63]. Public consultation started on 15 June 2026 and ran two months [64].
- **Xia Baolong in Hong Kong (from 16 June).** Xia began a two-day inspection visit [70].
- **US sanctions reshuffled (17 July).** See §3 [3].
- **Death of Tung Chee-hwa (8 September).** Tung Chee-hwa (董建華), Hong Kong's first Chief Executive, died aged 89. A state funeral was held on 20 September [86][85].
- **First Five-Year Plan and fifth Policy Address (16 September).** See §8 [49][52].
- **LegCo endorses both (9 October).** LegCo passed a motion supporting the Five-Year Plan and the motion of thanks for the Policy Address [62].
- **Election Committee nominations (7–20 October).** See §7 [72][73].
- **Fire inquiry report (22 October).** The Independent Committee on the Wang Fuk Court fire submitted its report to Lee [32].

## 6. The Wang Fuk Court fire (宏福苑大火) and its political effects

**The fire.**

- A fire broke out on 26 November 2025 at **Wang Fuk Court (宏福苑)**, a subsidised housing estate in Tai Po (大埔) that was under major renovation [31].
- It burned for 43 hours and destroyed seven of the estate's eight blocks [31].
- Court documents put the death toll at **168** [31].
- Reuters called it Hong Kong's deadliest fire in more than 75 years [29].
- The renovation project was worth HK$336 million [31].

**Criminal cases.**

- On 1 December 2025 Secretary for Security Chris Tang said police had tested 20 netting samples and seven did not meet fire-safety standards. Thirteen people had been arrested on suspicion of manslaughter [28].
- On 10 June 2026 seven people and two companies faced 25 charges, including manslaughter and conspiracy to defraud. The companies were consultant Will Power Architects and contractor Prestige Construction and Engineering [31].

**Speech-related arrests.**

- National security police arrested several people over the fire, including CUHK student Miles Kwan, who started a petition calling for accountability, and former district councillor Kenneth Cheung [30].
- Beijing's Office for Safeguarding National Security warned against "anti-China" elements exploiting the disaster [29][30].

**The inquiry.**

- On 12 December 2025 Lee set up an **Independent Committee** chaired by **Mr Justice David Lok**. Its members are Chan Kin-por (陳健波) and Dr Rex Auyeung Pak-kuen (歐陽伯權) [32][36].
- It began work on 19 December 2025 and held 30 days of evidential hearings with 80 witnesses. It collected more than 1 million files [32].
- It submitted its report on **22 October 2026** [32].
- According to unnamed sources, the report names four bodies: the Housing Bureau's **Independent Checking Unit (獨立審查組, ICU)**, which bears the most responsibility, plus the Buildings Department, the Fire Services Department and the Labour Department. The sources also say it names no bureau secretaries [34][35].
- The government will take legal advice before publishing it, because criminal trials are pending [33].

**Lee's statements.**

- On 14 January 2026, at the first sitting of the new LegCo, Lee pledged "full accountability" [44].
- After receiving the report he said the government would "shortly announce the plans for the comprehensive reforms and pursuing accountability" [33].
- In September 2026 he acknowledged "systemic and execution failures" [60].

**Rehousing the residents.**

- On 21 February 2026 the government offered to buy all flats in blocks A–G. The price was HK$8,000 per square foot for flats where the land premium had not been paid and HK$10,500 per square foot where it had. "Flat-for-flat" options were also offered [37].
- The full purchase was estimated at about HK$6.8 billion. About HK$2.8 billion would come from a donation fund and about HK$4 billion from public money. The government said the seven blocks would be demolished and the site would not be used again for housing [38].
- Block H, Wang Chi House (宏志閣), was added in July 2026 with HK$1 billion of extra funding [39].
- By 30 August 2026, 97.7% of owners (1,939 households) had accepted [40].

**The accountability debate.**

- Reporting in October 2026 said that, at that point, no senior official had resigned over the fire or apologised for it. Critics used the phrase 「刑不上局長」 ("punishment does not reach bureau chiefs") [89].
- Commentators in Taiwan's China Times and the World Journal describe how the fire's aftermath is handled as a test for Lee's possible second term. That is their analysis, not an announced condition from Beijing [68][69].

## 7. The 2027 Chief Executive election and John Lee

**Rules** (summarised by a Sing Tao column) [75]:

- The Election Committee has 1,500 members in five sectors of 300 each.
- A candidate needs at least 188 nominations, with at least 15 from each sector.
- The Candidate Eligibility Review Committee vets candidates.
- The winner needs more than 750 votes.
- The new Election Committee is constituted on 1 February 2027 for a term to 31 January 2032.
- The CE poll is expected in March 2027. As of 22 October 2026 the exact nomination period and polling date had not been gazetted.

The SCMP reports the CE election as due "next March" [72][73].

**Election Committee subsector elections, 22 November 2026.**

- Nominations ran from 7 to 20 October 2026 [72].
- 1,115 people are standing for 982 elected seats [73][74].
- The other members are 362 ex officio members and 156 members nominated by designated bodies [73]. Designated bodies nominated 161 individuals for those seats [74].
- 31 subsectors are contested, compared with 13 in 2021 [73].

**What Lee has said.**

- **15 June 2026, SCMP interview.** "A year is a long time in politics." He added: "I don't think I should consider this question now… time will come when I see." [65][66][64]
- **20 September 2026, on RTHK.** Asked whether he wanted to be the first CE to serve two full terms, he said the job requires a 「心無雜念」 ("uncluttered mind"). His task, he said, was to finish current work [61][60].
- He also said that whoever is CE in future must implement the Five-Year Plan [68].

**Reports and commentary** (unconfirmed by Beijing):

- Hong Kong Economic Times reporting, cited by the World Journal and China Times, says Beijing has been "收風" (gathering views) on Lee and his team. One source said Beijing is 「心中有數」 ("has a clear idea") about the next CE. The World Journal says no official confirmation exists [69][68].
- Political and business figures expect Lee's intentions to become clearer by the end of 2026 [69].
- Former LegCo President Rita Fan (范徐麗泰) said on radio in October 2026 that Lee was doing 「越做越好」 ("better and better"). She added that she does not know whether he will seek re-election [71].
- East Week (東周刊) reported rumours that someone may challenge the incumbent [76].
- A Macau commentary site argued that Lee, who turns 70 in 2027, is less likely to run. It named 「雙陳一鄧」 ("the two Chans and one Tang": Eric Chan, Paul Chan, Chris Tang) as possible contenders. This is the writer's speculation [77].
- The Straits Times quoted Bernard Chan (陳智思) saying Lee "served a purpose returning Hong Kong to law and order" [67].
- On LIHKG, users speculated about Paul Chan, Sun Dong, Chris Tang, and a return by Leung Chun-ying ("CY") [78]. "蛋餅強" in that thread appears to be a pun on 鄧炳強 (Chris Tang); this reading is inference [78].

## 8. The 2026 Policy Address and First Five-Year Plan (16 September 2026)

**Format.** Lee presented the **First Five-Year Plan for Economic and Social Development of the HKSAR (2026–2030)** at LegCo and then delivered the **2026 Policy Address** immediately after [51]. The government says the Policy Address is now the annual implementation report for the Plan [49][57]. Media describe it as the last Policy Address of his current term [58][67].

**The Five-Year Plan.**

- It was drawn up in the first year of the national 15th Five-Year Plan [49].
- It has 105 indicators, 22 of them "major indicators" [49].
- Its seven principles include "one country, two systems", a livelihood-oriented approach and an executive-led system [50].
- It sets no specific GDP growth target. Lee said this was because outside factors are beyond the government's control [60].
- BBC Chinese reported debate over whether the plan amounts to Beijing setting "KPIs" for Hong Kong [63].

**Four-year review given in the speech** [52]:

- GDP growth of 3.6% in 2025 and 5.1% year on year in the first half of 2026.
- Median monthly earnings up 20% over four years.
- Waiting time for subsidised rental housing cut from 6.1 to 4.8 years.
- Laws enacted on ride-hailing and on subdivided flats (Basic Housing Units Ordinance).

**Main measures** [53][55][56]:

- **"Four centres":**
  - a half-rate tax concession for physical commodity trading;
  - RMB-denominated, physically settled gold futures;
  - tests on tokenising Exchange Fund Bills;
  - a new Commissioner for AI post.
- **Northern Metropolis:**
  - three university towns, at San Tin, Hung Shui Kiu and Ta Kwu Ling, totalling over 1,000 hectares;
  - all nine New Development Areas started;
  - a bill offering 5% or half-rate profits tax for selected enterprises.
- **Housing:**
  - public housing output to average over 35,000 units a year in the five years from 2027-28;
  - all 30,000 Light Public Housing units completed by end-March 2027.
- **Births, 11 measures:**
  - newborn bonus of HK$20,000 extended for three years;
  - HK$30,000 for a second or later child;
  - stamp-duty waiver up to HK$20,000;
  - more IVF cycles, to 1,900 a year.
- **Youth:** a "30,000 Youth Employment & Internship Programme".
- **"AI City Brain" (AI城市大腦):**
  - an integrated city-management data system with an initial cost of HK$200 million [59];
  - a task force led by Chief Secretary Eric Chan;
  - first stage due in Q1 2028 and full capacity in 6–9 years [59].
  - Chan said it "isn't meant for surveillance" [59]. Asked whether it could be linked to the "SmartView" surveillance network for national security purposes, Lee said it is strictly for urban management [60].
- **Wang Fuk Court reforms** [54]:
  - a smoking ban on construction sites;
  - amendments to the Fire Services, Buildings and Building Management Ordinances;
  - a legal study on making bid-rigging (圍標) in building maintenance a crime;
  - government-vetted "pre-qualified lists" of consultants and contractors.
- **Civil service** [54]:
  - a Heads of Department Accountability System;
  - a two-tier administrative investigation mechanism;
  - a tighter performance appraisal system.

**Reception.** LegCo debated the Plan and the Policy Address together for three days and passed both motions on 9 October 2026 [62].

## 9. Public opinion and polling

- **Hong Kong Public Opinion Research Institute (香港民意研究所, PORI).** It used to publish regular CE ratings. Scholar Chung Kim-wah (鍾劍華) was placed on a HK$1 million wanted bounty in late 2024, and the institute he had been involved with then suspended all self-funded research indefinitely [80].
- **Chung Kim-wah's overseas survey.** His "海內外香港人民意研究計劃" published a survey on 17–18 August 2026. It was an online opt-in survey of 735 respondents taken August–November 2025, with 41% in the UK and 29% in Hong Kong. Lee scored an average of 0.3 out of 10 [80]. This is a self-selected sample.
- **Path of Democracy (民思政策研究所).** This institute, founded by ExCo member Ronny Tong, reported falls in its "One Country, Two Systems" index in March 2026. It said the fieldwork came right after the Jimmy Lai case and the Tai Po fire [81][80].
- **Street reception.** When Lee visited Yuen Chau Estate (元州邨) in Sham Shui Po on 26 August 2026, residents shouted 「下屆都係你做」 ("you'll do the next term too") [79].

## 10. Things that are easily confused

- **李家超 (John Lee Ka-chiu) vs 李嘉誠 (Li Ka-shing).** Li Ka-shing is the tycoon, widely nicknamed 「超人」 ("Superman"). Lee's nickname is 「超哥」 [7].
- **Chief Executive (行政長官) vs Chief Secretary for Administration (政務司司長).** The CS is the No. 2 official, currently **Eric Chan Kwok-ki (陳國基)**, who succeeded Lee in that post; Lee himself was CS from 2021 to 2022 [7].
- **陳國基 (Eric Chan, Chief Secretary) vs 陳茂波 (Paul Chan, Financial Secretary).** Commentators call them together "雙陳" ("the two Chans") [77]. A third Chan sits on ExCo as a non-official member: Chan Kin-por (陳健波), who is also on the fire inquiry panel [10][36].
- **Executive Council (行政會議) vs Legislative Council (立法會).** ExCo advises the CE in private. LegCo is the elected legislature, presided over by Starry Lee [10][42]. Before 1997, ExCo was called 行政局.
- **Five-Year Plan vs Policy Address.** The Plan is a 2026–2030 blueprint. The Policy Address is the annual programme [49][57]. Hong Kong's "first" five-year plan is a different document from the national 15th Five-Year Plan [49].
- **國安委 vs 國安公署.** 國安委 is the HKSAR committee chaired by Lee. 國安公署 is the Office for Safeguarding National Security, sent by Beijing and headed by Dong Jingwei [83].
- **港澳辦 vs 中聯辦.** 港澳辦 (Xia Baolong) is the office in Beijing. 中聯辦 (Zhou Ji) is the liaison office in Hong Kong [84][85].
- **Wang Fuk Court (宏福苑) vs Wang Chi House (宏志閣).** Wang Chi House is block H of the same estate. It was largely unaffected by the fire and was added to the buy-out later [37][39].
- **Macau.** Macau's Chief Executive is Sam Hou Fai (岑浩輝) [90]. Posts saying "特首" can refer to him.
- **"John Lee" elsewhere.** The name is common in English. Hong Kong posts mean 李家超 when they mention the Chief Executive.
