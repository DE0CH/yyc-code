# Background brief: Hong Kong's Election Committee, the 2026 Election Committee Subsector Ordinary Elections, and the 2027 Chief Executive election

*Facts as of 22 October 2026. Numbers in square brackets refer to the source list at the end.*

---

## 1. What the subject is

The **Election Committee** (選舉委員會) is a 1,500-member body in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) of the People's Republic of China. Its legal functions are to nominate candidates for and elect the **Chief Executive** (行政長官, the head of the HKSAR government), and to nominate Legislative Council (立法會, LegCo) candidates and elect 40 of LegCo's 90 members [1]. The Chief Executive it elects is then appointed by the Central People's Government in Beijing [4]. Each Election Committee has a five-year term [1][4].

The Election Committee is not elected by the general public. Its members come in three ways [1]:

- **Ex-officio members** (當然委員): people who hold specified offices (for example all LegCo members, and Hong Kong's deputies to the National People's Congress and members of the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference). 362 seats.
- **Members nominated by designated bodies** (指定團體提名委員): 156 seats.
- **Elected members** (選任委員): 982 seats, chosen in the **Election Committee Subsector Elections** (選舉委員會界別分組選舉) by a small, mostly corporate electorate [1][3][17].

The **2026 Election Committee Subsector Ordinary Elections** (2026年選舉委員會界別分組一般選舉) fill the 982 elected seats for the next term. Polling day is **Sunday 22 November 2026**, 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. [1]. The new Election Committee's term runs from **1 February 2027 to 31 January 2032** [1][3]. Its first major task is the **2027 Chief Executive election**, which the government says will be held in **March 2027** [11][17]. The incumbent Chief Executive, John Lee Ka-chiu (李家超), finishes his term on 30 June 2027 [10].

**Convener system.** Annex I creates a "system of conveners" (召集人制度). The chief convener (總召集人) must be an Election Committee member who holds an office of state leadership, and designates conveners for each sector [4].

## 4. Rules for the Chief Executive election

From Annex I of the Basic Law (as amended in 2021) and the Basic Law itself:

- A candidate must be nominated by **at least 188 Election Committee members**, with **at least 15 from each of the five sectors**. Each member may nominate only one candidate [4].
- The Election Committee votes by secret ballot, one person one vote. The winner must obtain **more than 750 votes** [4].
- The **Candidate Eligibility Review Committee** (候選人資格審查委員會, 資審會, CERC) reviews and confirms the eligibility of candidates for both Election Committee membership and Chief Executive. The **Committee for Safeguarding National Security of the HKSAR** (香港特別行政區維護國家安全委員會), based on review by the Police's national security department, gives an opinion on whether a candidate upholds the Basic Law and swears allegiance to the HKSAR. No legal proceedings may be brought against CERC decisions made on that opinion [4].
- The Chief Executive must be a Chinese citizen aged at least 40, a Hong Kong permanent resident with no right of abode in any foreign country, and ordinarily resident in Hong Kong for at least 20 continuous years [47].
- The term is five years; a Chief Executive may serve **not more than two consecutive terms** [47].
- The Chief Executive is appointed by the Central People's Government [4][47].

## 5. History and background

### Before 2021

- **1996**: a 400-member Selection Committee (推選委員會), set up by the NPC's HKSAR Preparatory Committee, chose the first Chief Executive and the Provisional Legislative Council (tertiary source) [33].
- **1998 and 2000**: Election Committees (800 members) chose some LegCo seats; from 2001 the Chief Executive Election Ordinance (Cap. 569, 《行政長官選舉條例》) governed the body (tertiary source) [33].
- **2002, 2005 and 2007** CE elections: 800-member committee. **2012 and 2017**: 1,200 members in 38 subsectors after the 2010 reform (tertiary source) [33].
- Before 2021, professional subsectors such as education were contested between pro-democracy and pro-establishment candidates, with seats elected by individual voters; those seats are now chosen by corporate voting [16].
- Election Committee terms normally began on **1 February** of the year a Chief Executive's term expires (for example 1 February 2007, 2012 and 2017) (tertiary source) [33]. Cap. 569 now states that the Election Committee "shall be constituted on 1 February in the year in which the term of office of the Chief Executive is to expire" [6][3].

### The 2021 overhaul ("improving the electoral system")

- **11 March 2021**: the National People's Congress adopted a Decision on improving Hong Kong's electoral system, under which the Election Committee would be expanded to 1,500 members from five sectors and given the tasks of electing the CE designate and part of LegCo and nominating candidates for both [5].
- **30 March 2021**: the NPC Standing Committee amended Annex I (CE selection) of the Basic Law, creating the current structure [4]. The Election Committee grew from 1,200 to 1,500 seats, with a new Fifth Sector [5][16]. The Commercial (third) subsector replaced the Hong Kong Chinese Enterprises Association subsector, Technology and innovation replaced Information technology, Education merged the old Education and Higher education subsectors, and the district-committee, mainland-associations and national-organisations subsectors were new [5].
- **12 August 2021**: nominations for the 2021 subsector elections closed with 1,056 nomination forms; only 13 of 40 subsectors were contested [32]. An HK01 tally that day counted 879 seats it regarded as under the effective control of Beijing and the HKSAR government, more than half of 1,500 [32].
- **19 September 2021**: the 2021 Election Committee Subsector Ordinary Elections. 4,889 registered voters; 4,389 voted by 6 p.m., a turnout of 89.77% [29][28]. In 23 subsectors seats were filled uncontested, with more than 600 people returned automatically [16][12].
- **22 October 2021**: the current (sixth) Election Committee was constituted; its term ran to **21 October 2026** [6]. This was outside the usual 1 February cycle.
- **19 December 2021**: LegCo general election, the first in which the Election Committee constituency elected 40 seats [51][33].

### The 2022 Chief Executive election

- Polling was postponed from 27 March to **8 May 2022** because of a worsening COVID-19 outbreak, and the nomination period moved to 3 to 16 April 2022 [39].
- John Lee Ka-chiu, former Chief Secretary for Administration and a former police officer, was the only candidate. He received **1,416 votes** out of 1,428 members who voted; 8 voted against [31][30]. China's Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office said he won 99.16% of the vote [31]. BBC Chinese reported commentary comparing the race to Macau's "one-person mode" (一人模式) [31].
- Lee took office on 1 July 2022 [31].

### 2025

- **2 April 2025**: the then Secretary for Constitutional and Mainland Affairs, Erick Tsang Kwok-wai (曾國衞), introduced the Electoral Legislation (Miscellaneous Amendments) Bill 2025 to prepare for the September 2025 by-election and the December 2025 LegCo election [44].
- **7 September 2025**: Election Committee Subsector By-elections (選委會界別分組補選). 28 candidates contested 21 seats in 6 subsectors; the whole by-election filled 93 vacant seats. 3,534 voters cast ballots (provisional turnout 97.33%); polling went ahead in a typhoon. In the Architectural, surveying, planning and landscape subsector two candidates tied on 26 votes and the seat was decided by drawing lots [27]. The budget exceeded HK$200 million [27].
- **26 November 2025**: a five-alarm fire at **Wang Fuk Court** (宏福苑) in Tai Po, under major renovation, affected 7 of its 8 blocks and killed 168 people; Lee set up an independent committee to investigate [36]. EAC chairman Justice David Lok also chairs that inquiry [16]. The committee submitted its report to the Chief Executive on **22 October 2026** [43].
- **7 December 2025**: LegCo general election. Geographical constituency turnout was 31.9% [42]. The Election Committee constituency had an electorate of 1,466 members [48].

## 7. The 2026 contest in detail

### Nominations

- 1,115 candidate nomination forms for 982 elected seats in 36 subsectors; 40 designated-body forms covering 161 nominees for the 156 nominated seats [2][13].
- **31 of the 36 elective subsectors are oversubscribed** and will vote; 5 have exactly as many candidates as seats [12][17]. In 2021, 13 subsectors were contested [14].
- The five uncontested subsectors are Education, Sports/performing arts/culture/publication, Associations of Chinese fellow townsmen, Labour, and Representatives of Hong Kong members of relevant national organisations, together 259 elected seats. If their candidates pass vetting and none withdraw, they are elected without a vote [12][14][13].
- All 18 First Sector subsectors are contested, ending their long practice of coordinating lists to avoid contests. Commercial (second) and Industrial (second) hold contested polls for the first time [14]. First Sector nominations totalled 345, about 14% more than last time (as reported by Hong Kong Commercial Daily) [20].
- Fiercest contests: **Social welfare, 25 candidates for 15 seats**; **Medical and health services, 23 for 15** [2][12].
- Other contested examples: Agriculture and fisheries 72 for 60; Grassroots associations 75 for 60; Hong Kong and Kowloon district committees 88 for 76; New Territories district committees 93 for 80; Heung Yee Kuk 30 for 27 [2][12][20]. Agriculture and fisheries and the Heung Yee Kuk were uncontested last time [20].
- Average competition is 1.14 candidates per seat [13].
- About 60% (587) of members elected in 2021 are seeking re-election; about one in four of the 1,276 nominees (336) nominated John Lee in 2022 [13].

### Electorate

- The final register lists 9,342 subsector voters (3,448 individuals, 5,894 corporate bodies) [24].
- Excluding the uncontested subsectors, **5,757 voters** are eligible to vote on 22 November, mostly corporate bodies [13][14]. For comparison, the geographical constituency register has 4,116,965 electors [24].

### What happens next

1. CERC vetting, with advice from the Committee for Safeguarding National Security; validly nominated candidates are gazetted within 14 days of 20 October [13][46].
2. Uncontested subsectors are declared elected after vetting [13].
3. Campaign: candidates may send one postage-free election mail to each voter; election advertisements must be lodged for public inspection within three working days [3].
4. Polling on 22 November: voters must show the original HKID card or a specified alternative (a BN(O) passport is not accepted). Voters shade ovals for up to the number of seats in their subsector. Ballot-checking machines, used voluntarily, warn of unmarked or likely invalid papers but do not record choices. Ballots are counted electronically at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre in Wan Chai [1][3].
5. If the Electronic Poll Register system fails, officials will spend 15 minutes investigating before deciding whether to switch to printed registers, according to Chief Electoral Officer Herman Tse (謝衍文) [8][9].
6. About 1,000 civil servants will work on polling day [3][10].

## 8. The 2027 Chief Executive election: current state

- **Timing**: Lee said on 28 July 2026 that the CE election will be held in March 2027 [11]. China Daily and SCMP also report March [17][14]. The exact polling day and nomination period had not been announced in the sources found.
- **Electorate**: the seventh Election Committee, which takes office 1 February 2027 [1].
- **Budget**: HK$81 million [25].
- **The incumbent's position**: John Lee has not said whether he will seek a second term.
  - In an SCMP interview published on 15 June 2026 he said "a year is a long time in politics" and "I don't think I should consider this question now" [34].
  - On 20 September 2026, asked on RTHK whether he hoped to be the first CE to complete two terms, he did not answer directly and said a Chief Executive must be "心無雜念" ("free of distracting thoughts") [36].
  - Executive Council member Ronny Tong Ka-wah (湯家驊) told Bloomberg he did not "detect any unwillingness on his part to continue" [35].
  - Bernard Chan (陳智思), a former Hong Kong NPC deputy and Executive Council convener under Carrie Lam, suggested signals might emerge after the Communist Party Central Committee's autumn plenary session [35].
- **Reports of assessment**: Taiwan's United Daily News, citing Hong Kong Economic Times and other media, reported that Beijing was gathering views on Lee and his team, and that people in politics and business expected his intentions to become clearer around the end of 2026. UDN said these were media reports with no official confirmation from Beijing [36]. The same report listed the handling of and accountability for the Wang Fuk Court fire as a key test [36].
- **Historical pattern**: no Chief Executive has served two full terms since 1997 [35][36]. Tung Chee-hwa (董建華) resigned in March 2005 partway through his second term; Donald Tsang (曾蔭權) completed that term and was re-elected in 2007; Leung Chun-ying (梁振英) and Carrie Lam (林鄭月娥) each served one term and did not seek a second [37][35].
- **Lee's record as presented in the news**: Hong Kong's first Five-Year Plan for Economic and Social Development (2026–2030) (香港特別行政區經濟和社會發展第一個五年規劃), released in September 2026 alongside his 2026 Policy Address, his fifth and last of the current term [36][35]; enactment of Article 23 national security legislation [37][34]. Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office director Xia Baolong (夏寶龍) made a two-day inspection visit to Hong Kong in June 2026; Lee thanked Xia for his recognition of the government's work [37].
- **Other names**: Regina Ip Lau Suk-yee (葉劉淑儀), Executive Council convenor, who failed to secure enough nominations in 2012 and 2017, said in April 2026 she would "definitely not" run in 2027, citing age [38]. No other candidate had declared in the sources found.
- **Columnists' questions**: a Sing Tao column noted that recent elections have allowed "free competition" and asked whether the 2027 CE race would allow competition rather than the "one-person mode" [18]. A business source quoted by Sing Tao said Election Committee membership used to make members a "key minority" whose votes contenders had to seek, but that its "gold content" (含金量) has fallen and it is now mostly a status symbol [19].

## 9. Ongoing issues, debates and controversies

**Competition and its management.**
- Sing Tao's political column reported that representatives of several sectors had been "gently reminded" (溫提) to take part and raise competition, that the authorities did not want many uncontested seats, and that there would be no "blessed list". Once approved candidates pass nomination, they may compete freely "without undermining the unity of the patriotic camp" [18].
- A later column reported that some current members had decided not to run after "friendly communication", including in the Fifth Sector; that people over 80 were being thanked and encouraged to make way; and that "the real reshuffle began before the nomination period" [19]. The column also noted that Senior Counsel Anthony Neoh (梁定邦), who turns 80 in November, had registered for the Legal subsector [19].
- SCMP quoted observers: Lau Siu-kai (劉兆佳), consultant to the Chinese Association of Hong Kong and Macao Studies (全國港澳研究會), said competition would improve members' performance; Sonny Lo Shiu-hing (盧兆興) said Beijing wanted to widen the "patriotic front" and that competition remained confined to the elite while the public stayed apathetic or cynical [14].
- Pro-Beijing papers (a Ta Kung Pao editorial reprinted on the Hong Kong and Macao Work Office's website; Hong Kong Commercial Daily) describe the nominations as evidence of "healthy competition" and "high-quality democracy" and reject the "all one colour" and "no choice" criticism [21][20].

**Small electorate and corporate voting.** Only 5,757 voters can vote in contested subsectors [13]. Since 2021, professional seats once elected by individual professionals are chosen by corporate voters [16]. The Election Committee has long been criticised for its "small-circle" base and composition favouring pro-Beijing and business interests [45]. No opposition-leaning candidates are standing [14].

**Vetting.** All candidates are vetted by the CERC on national security advice; CERC decisions on that basis cannot be challenged in court [4][13].

**Term gap.** The sixth Election Committee's term legally ended on 21 October 2026 and the seventh begins on 1 February 2027. The EAC's guidelines state that the current final register of members remains valid until the next one is published on 1 February 2027 [6].

**Cost.** The 2026 elections are budgeted at HK$260 million: HK$135 million for staff, HK$9 million for publicity and HK$116 million for other costs [10][25]. The 2025 by-election cost over HK$200 million for 21 contested seats; the EAC chairman said venues had to be booked before it was known how many seats would be contested [27].

**Polling hours and technology.** Polling hours are 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., down from 7:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. in 2021, following legislators' suggestions [10]. Lawmakers asked the government to strengthen its systems to avoid a repeat of the system failure in the 2023 District Council election [10].

**A LegCo vacancy.** Lee said on 28 July 2026 that, given timing, workload and "relative necessity", the government would first hold the three scheduled elections before considering a by-election for a vacant LegCo seat [11].

**International comment.** The UK government's six-monthly report on Hong Kong, published 22 October 2026, records invalid and blank vote figures for the December 2025 LegCo election [42]. The HKSAR government strongly condemned the report on 23 October [20].

## 10. Things easily confused with this subject

| Item | How it differs |
|---|---|
| **Election Committee constituency of LegCo** (選舉委員會界別, 選委界) | One of LegCo's three types of constituency: 40 LegCo seats elected *by* the Election Committee members. Last elected 7 December 2025 with a 1,466-member electorate [48][13]. Next in 2029 [13]. |
| **Functional constituencies** (功能界別) | 28 LegCo constituencies with their own register (190,848 voters in 2026), separate from the EC subsector register (9,342) [24]. Several share names with EC subsectors. |
| **ECSS by-elections** (界別分組補選) | Fill vacant elected EC seats mid-term (last on 7 September 2025) [27]. The 2026 poll is an *ordinary* election (一般選舉) for the whole new term [1]. |
| **Electoral Affairs Commission** (選舉管理委員會, 選管會, EAC) | The independent statutory body that runs elections, not the Election Committee. Chairman: Mr Justice David Lok Kai-hong (陸啟康), High Court judge, term 1 Sep 2025–31 Aug 2028. Members: Professor Daniel Shek and Bernard Man SC [26][3]. |
| **Registration and Electoral Office** (選舉事務處, REO) | Government department under CMAB that supports the EAC; Chief Electoral Officer Herman Tse [3][9]. |
| **Constitutional and Mainland Affairs Bureau** (政制及內地事務局, CMAB) | Policy bureau for elections; Secretary Janice Tse Siu-wa (謝小華) since 30 March 2026 [41][9]. |
| **Candidate Eligibility Review Committee** (資審會, CERC) | Vets candidates; separate from all of the above [4]. |
| **Chief Executive election vs. Election Committee election** | The November 2026 vote chooses the electors; the March 2027 vote chooses the Chief Executive [1][11]. |
| **District Council election** (區議會選舉) | Separate election due by the end of 2027 [11]. |
| **Selection Committee** (推選委員會, 1996) | The 400-member body that chose the first CE; a predecessor [33]. |
| **Macau's Chief Executive Election Committee** | A separate body in the Macau SAR; posts compare single-candidate races in the two cities ("one-person mode") [31]. |
| **"Election Committee" in other jurisdictions** | The name is generic; posts about elections bodies elsewhere (for example Taiwan's Central Election Commission 中央選舉委員會) are unrelated. |
| **Numbering of terms** | The Election Committee elected in 2026 is described as the seventh term (2027–2032) (tertiary source) [33]; the CE elected in 2027 will be the seventh-term Chief Executive [33]. |
